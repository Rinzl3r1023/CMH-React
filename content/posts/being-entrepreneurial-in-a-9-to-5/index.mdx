## **You Don't Have to Be an Entrepreneur to Be an Entrepreneur**

Wait, what?

Chris, I'm not a startup founder and I've never even attempted to launch my own venture, how am I an entrepreneur?

I hear you.

Loud and clear...

Here's what I mean by that.

Every week I talk to someone that goes out of their way to reiterate to me they're not an entrepreneur and thus, could provide little-to-no value to my podcast, Entrepreneur Hour.

You see, we began to reach out beyond the straight-forward entrepreneurs to gather insights and feedback from folks to include on the show.

Why?

Because everyone is somewhere along their journey and that doesn't mean it's any less valuable.

In fact, our downloads indicate the opposite.

We have friends of the podcast that reach as high as global icons to #1 New York Times Best Selling Authors but do you know what episodes get just as many, if not more, downloads?

That's right, the common guy that has yet to even start a business.

I have my theories as to why but they primarily revolve around relatability.

Daymond John or Tony Robbins now operate in a different stratosphere than you and me.

And while we most certainly respect and admire their accolades and achievements, we can't really fully feel their story like we can the young, 20-something that's still trying to figure out this thing we call life.

And that's where the magic happens...

When we see someone else living out their journey and are able to encapsulate that moment in time, others can see themselves in those stories.

And that's what I loved about Britt and his story.

You see, Britt could walk around with his head down, complaining about a 9 to 5 and not "doing his own thing," as I so often here.

He could tell me and others he's not an entrepreneur.

But, let me ask you, when DO we become entrepreneurs?

For example, you know when you were a child and celebrated your birthday?

Grandparents and parents alike would ask you a silly but applicable question to our scenario...

Something like, "do you feel any different now that you're ____?"

Naturally, the answer was always a resounding no, at least it was for me.

But imagine for a second we take that same logic and apply it to entrepreneurship.

The minute you file to incorporate your business, you don't bust out the front door and scream that you're now an entrepreneur.

Why?

Because entrepreneurship is a lifestyle, not a career.

#TheEnd

Not quite, hang with me...

The reason I say you're an entrepreneur even when you're not an entrepreneur is because you, like Britt, are probably already on that path!

I trace my roots back to my youth, just like Britt.

For example, when Britt was mowing lawns at 12, does that not technically make him an entrepreneur or at least demonstrates entrepreneurial tendencies?

I would 100% argue YES!

Something innate inside of Britt makes him an entrepreneur, not just the status of his current employment arrangement.

And here's why that matters...

## **We Miss Opportunities to Grow When We Falsely Mislabel Ourselves**

That, right there...read it over again and remind yourself of this on a daily basis.

Why?

Because I believe it's one of the biggest reasons we not only keep ourselves small but also fail to capitalize on our current opportunities.

Britt has a job..so?

Britt is taking advantage of this amazing, incredible opportunity to not only expand his skill sets but make invaluable connections that could largely lead to his future successes as an entrepreneur, if he so chooses.

Wouldn't you say being a GM of a multi-million dollar business is going to help in SOME way in the future?

And yet, we often find ourselves being critical of our current circumstances because they're not the pot of gold at the end of the rainbow.

We don't have an Instagram audience of over 1 million...

We don't have the corner office at our own company on the top floor.

But here's the thing, everyone started somewhere.

Why not take full advantage of every situation in your life?

I have a rule for myself...

I don't commit fully to things I know I can't deliver 10,000% of my effort.

Now, I help people in auxiliary capacities but if I give someone my word that I'm committing to something...ANYTHING...You're getting my best.

And that's a principle I would encourage each of you to adopt if you haven't already.

Unlike Britt, I had to learn that much later in life.

I, too, moped around about my circumstances, dreaming of a different life but not recognizing the amazing opportunities to grow right in front of my face.

And then I started my first business and realized how much each and every one of those situations helped me along my new path.

Wow, did I wish I had paid more attention and just enjoyed and maximized the ride instead of complaining to myself and others.

Maybe you're in college and you HATE going to class, much as I did.

Maybe you constantly ask yourself how you're EVER even going to use "X" in the real world.

I would challenge you to shift your focus on what you CAN apply to your future, not what you CAN'T.

What can you learn today that is going to better equip you for tomorrow?

And that is what Britt has learned and why he'll now succeed at anything he chooses in life.

## **The Dark Side of the Moon...**

Look, I've given you the rah-rah spiel but now I want to have a more serious conversation with you, so hang with me for just a few more minutes.

Recent statistics have shown over 85% of people are currently unhappy with their careers.

Yes, 85%!

Now, think about that for a second...

We graduate high school or college and enter into the workforce by the age of ~20 (For the purposes of this discussion).

If we're lucky, we MIGHT be able to retire by the age of 65...and all of that is assuming something like Social Security will still be in place at that point (a HUGE IF).

Well, the average life expectancy is currently 78.7 years (as of 2018).

By my math, in an absolutely ideal world that gives us 33.7 years not working and 45 years in the work force.

All of this keeping in mind that your early years include schooling.

We're going to count that as "work" years: So five of those 20 years are now going in our "Not Working" bucket.

**Scoreboard Reads:** 18.7 Years Not Working VS. 60 Years Working

By those metrics, you're looking at 76.23% of your entire life in the work force.

One third of all that time is spent sleeping, don't forget to calculate that into the equation.

Go ahead and reduce 6.23 years from your already meager 18.7 and you're now down to 12.47.

Seeing where we're going here?

Try on only 15.94% of your waking life, non-work life, NOT working!

***Yes, I'm not counting for nights and weekends during work years. But ask yourself, how many nights and weekends do you spend stressed about work just before you wake up and do it all over again?**

Also, keep in mind this is all assuming you can actually retire at 65, while current statistics show 66% of millennials have absolutely nothing saved for retirement and somewhere around 78% of all people don't even have $2,000 to fall back on in an emergency.

Depressed yet?

Here's why I'm begging you to really consider these facts.

This isn't some cliche, rallying cry to not take life for granted but rather one to really think about what you're doing with your life and is it really what you actually want to be doing?

Not all of us can be entrepreneurs...Maybe we're just not wired that way.

That's okay!

Most of us nut balls barely even sniff success amongst the stockpile of failures under our belts.

We largely belong in looney bins.

But dammit we're going down doing what we love and that quality is one I wish I could share with all of my fellow brothers and sisters.

We're so afraid to fully engage in what we're doing or take the necessary risk to do something we actually enjoy doing with this one life we've been given.

We spend our meager 12.47 waking years bitching and complaining about the 60 years we spend doing said job we hate.

I was a severely depressed person in college and the years, thereafter.

I found asylum in my faith and in entrepreneurship.

I'm not saying it's for everyone and I certainly know it's not the remedy to all of life's ailments.

But what I do know is it aligns with my soul and there's too many of us not desperately seeking that out en lieu of security or complacency.

There's too many of us miserable and living for the weekend so we can bludgeon our faces and poison our bodies with tequila shots and skin-deep relationships to drown out the void in our lives that comes with not getting everything you can out of this life.

So, when I see someone like Britt that has figured it out at such an early age, it's most certainly worth having on my show...

## **The Early Roots In Entrepreneurship**

Britt Douglas has never known anything but the entrepreneurial path. Having started his own lawn mowing business around the age of 12, Britt soon realized his efforts can translate into serious money and the freedom to own his day and be his own boss.

Soon thereafter at around the age of 16, Britt realized his love and passion for automobiles. Pursuing that passion, he began "flipping cars," where this eventually led to his current position as GM of Worldwide Vintage Autos by the ripe old age of 23!

Britt largely credits his exposure to the entrepreneurial mindset as the reason he was able to reach such heights in a business where he's largely working with people two to three times his age.
