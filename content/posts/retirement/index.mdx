## **I Don't Like Golf...**

My dad says this all the time.

Some of his buddies and neighbors play literally every single day and often times have tried to get him to join in.

But at the end of the day, that's just not in some people's blood.

It's certainly not the case in my family.

And so my father chooses to provide value to a local small business.

In fact, since he's gotten involved, they've seen a tremendous spike in business.

The gentleman he works with is about my age or somewhere in his early 30's...

And it just works.

Where my father's understanding of utilizing the internet to drive traffic and business falls a bit short, he more than makes up for it with his ability to essentially sell ice to an Eskimo in his sleep.

In fact, to this day I've yet to discover a better salesman than my father.

**Spoiler Alert:** I meet a lot of business people and entrepreneurs so that's high praise, not being biased here.

I've worked with my dad since as early as I can remember.

Anytime I had the opportunity to go with him to work, I jumped at the opportunity.

I wanted to know how things worked.

How they made what he sold.

Meet the people behind the scenes.

They were just common folks but to me they were all larger than life.

I watched him flourish.

Winning something like salesman of the year for nine consecutive years and generating a whopping $48MM dollars in sales throughout his tenure.

You bring someone almost $50MM and they better throw you a damn parade when you retire.

I wasn't present for that sendoff party...but I've seen the pictures and heard the stories.

It's one of those mind is still willing but the body rebukes scenarios.

I can only imagine what he went through.

He was ready...but then again not so ready.

Like closing a chapter in your life and also knowing you're that much closer to the retirement home.

And I don't mean to sound morbid, just imagining what must go through the mind and what will most assuredly go through my own.

Being the self-starter he was and is, he opted to launch his own auto brokerage.

We've talked about this in previous installments here.

And to a large degree he experienced early success!

Generating something like $100,000 in revenues.

But soon after, the body again reminded him of his actual age.

We watched him suffer and struggle mightily.

While we supported his entrepreneurial spirit, it was just becoming time to let someone else carry the load.

Jumping into a startup is hard...hardest thing I've ever done.

Doing so in your early 70's in borderline lunacy.

So, what to do?

## **An Unexpected Opportunity**

As it turned out, a neighboring competitor had taken notice of my father's little shop.

Admiring the way he did business and his ridiculous connections, he began to plant some seeds of strategic partnership.

Just a few trades here and there...nothing serious or permanent.

In this particular situation, the young chap had the funding from his father, whom had yet to retire as a pilot but had envisioned a retirement partnership with his son.

With no prior business experience and an accounting degree, it's safe to say someone with 40+ years of business experience could provide just a slight bit of value, right!? (HA!)

Little did he know at the time, my father is everything I say he is.

Recently, I dug through some old documents of his and found where he had been featured in newspapers, asked to come speak at events and recognized for his career achievements.

These were all dated back to his "prime," so to speak.

Around that time, he opted to move to Dayton, OH to join a small little woodworking business, different company than the one I mentioned prior, after leaving his longtime home in Dallas, TX.

And within just a few short years he had risen to VP of Sales, managing hundreds of people and involved with growth from something like $3MM to $80MM in revenues.

In other words, this local auto competitor guy basically had Michael Jordan living down the street and didn't even know it.

[via GIPHY](https://giphy.com/gifs/chicago-bulls-95-96-72-10-26BRyD3KiIvaRPluM)

I say that jokingly but to say this guy needed my dad is quite literally the understatement of the century.

And so it went, after months of collaboration and a swap here or there, he eventually asked my dad to become more involved and consider just doing his own thing on the side.

Now, my dad works when he can.

He suffers from a debilitating disease called Crohn's, most likely from all the years of ridiculous work, stress and between meeting eating habits.

And so this arrangement is perfect for him.

It's perfect for the young gent he works with.

It's a win-win-win.

## **The Idea of a Wisdom Worker**

We can all provide value.

When you're younger and, let's say less experienced, it's usually in the form of hard work.

The hustle and grinders, right?

But when you get older that's just not in the cards.

And nor should it be.

Because truth be told, the point of the hustle and grind is to just accumulate knowledge and expertise.

If you're just hustling for the sake of hustling, you're no better off than the Tasmanian Devil from the old cartoons.

**I've already reached my GIF quota for this post but you know exactly who I'm talking about.*

It's futile and unrealistic to keep up that pace.

And what you'll quickly learn is that a calculated approach, backed my know-how and wisdom, is the path to greatest success.

So, while you're spinning around in a cloud of dust, why not surround yourself with your own Yoda to point all of those efforts in the right direction.

I've talked at length about my own business mentors and my dear friend/coach/advisor, Bill Douglas.

Sometimes I get so fixated on an idea, barreling down the road and hellbent to make it happen, but Bill can stop me dead in my tracks with a simple "why?"

As in, why are you doing this in the first place?

Or, how do you envision this working out in six months?

Mind you, these aren't direct examples but you get my drift.

And herein lies the problem.

When we don't have a Wisdom Worker around us, we find a huge gap in the "what we don't know" category.

But most entrepreneurs would scoff at this idea.

They want an army of young trailblazers around them.

Ready to go sprint together and revolutionize an industry.

And so this notion of a Wisdom Worker is devalued.

At least by those not seeing it for the Ace Up the Sleeve it is.

So my goal, as it is with most of these diatribes you read on the site, is to change your perspective.

Not entirely, just a subtle shift.

Truth be told, sometimes that's all we need.

It's not that we're clueless.

In fact, often times the path we're headed down makes all the sense in the world.

Research, brainstorm sessions, meetings + competitive analysis all indicated this "thing" you're onto makes all the sense in the world.

That this new initiative is the one to change the game.

You may be right, but why not validate that with someone that's been in the saddle for a few rodeos?

Why not get some perspective from someone that's already walked down your path and can dust off the old map from somewhere buried in the closet?

Business is funny...

When they say history repeats itself, I wouldn't say it's entirely true but it most certainly leaves clues.

Think of it...

One day someone had this wild idea to strap two wings on a missile and load people into it.

Can you imagine hearing that pitch for the first time?

Now, I know the Boeing we see today is not the first aircraft to take flight.

But talk about a modern marvel at that time.

Talk about an undertaking and an ambitious thought.

And now we're talking about building tunnels underground and blasting people all over the world.

In just a few short years, we're going to be sending Bullet Trains (They call them that for a reason) through submerged hamster wheels!

What lies just below the ground you walk on at this very moment will be a highway for super trains moving hundreds of miles per hour.

Insane...

And so you get my point.

There's always some new iteration of something.

And those revolutionary ideas leave clues behind for us to follow...that is, if we're smart enough to look.

Now, I obviously don't expect you to go find one of the Wright Brothers to serve as your tour guide.

But you better bet your bottom dollar the folks over at HyperLoop would if they could.

And what's more is this doesn't even HAVE to be some grandiose enterprise you're launching.

It could just be a little old automotive dealership in the small town of Tyrone, GA that just aims to put food on the table and provide value to their local community.

They say knowledge is power.

I challenge you to put that one to the test.
