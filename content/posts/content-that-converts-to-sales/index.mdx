Let's talk about overwhelm.

If you're running an online business, you already know the complexity is real — the tool stack, the moving parts, leading a team you rarely see face to face. AI has made a lot of that easier. But when you layer marketing on top, something quietly goes wrong: marketing becomes something you *do* instead of something you're *intentional* about. You find yourself just… making content. I've been in that exact cycle, so I'm not judging.

Here's the shift I want you to see. We've left the era where the whole game was convincing people to make content at all — that was a 2016 problem, and to his credit, that's the wave Gary Vee rode when it was exactly what people needed to hear. That's not the problem anymore. We're now in a differentiation era (call it the Hormozi era): everybody knows to post, so the question is how you stand out in a crowded market. This post is about exactly that — how to create content that converts to sales using a simple map my wife and business partner Kim built. Let's get into it.

## **Why Does My Content Get Engagement but No Sales?**

Because most content stops at value and never builds the bridge to your offer.

It's easy to give free tips that get likes. What most coaches skip is *deepening the relationship* and walking someone toward a next step. Engagement feels like progress, but if the content never connects the problem your audience has to the solution you sell, it just sits there as entertainment. Differentiation matters here too: in a crowded market, even people who are aware of their problem are hearing the same advice from a hundred accounts. Standing out isn't about being eccentric or doing gimmicky "virality" stunts — it's about showcasing your unique ability and mechanism so the right person sees why *you're* the qualified guide.

## **What Is the Problem Solution Content Map?**

It's a simple planning tool — created by Kim — that maps your audience's real problems to solution-driven content that moves them toward your offer. It's part of our **Content to Customers** method, which runs in three phases: **Capture** (content that attracts, connects, and converts), **Monetize** (sales assets like VSLs, sales pages, and webinars), and **Advertise** (putting ad spend behind proven winners). The map lives in phase one.

The idea is a bridge. On one side of the canyon is your audience's problem. On the other side is where they want to be — and your product or service is what gets them across. Each piece of content is a plank in that bridge: it proves your expertise, differentiates you, and gives them an "aha" — a new way of seeing the problem — so they're actually open to a new solution. If you don't give people that shift first, they're rarely ready to consider what you sell.

## **How Do You Build a Problem Solution Content Map?**

You map four simple things: the problem, where they are in the journey, the solutions, and the bridge to your offer. Here's the process:

1. **List your ideal client's real problems.** Don't overthink market-sophistication theory — just name what they're actually struggling with. (For our audience, it's overwhelm: they've tried a lot, spent a lot, and still feel stuck.)
2. **For each problem, map 1–4 solutions.** Each solution becomes its own piece of content.
3. **Make each solution prove expertise and differentiate you** — a mechanism or approach that's distinctly yours, not generic tips.
4. **End each piece with a clear next step** that leads into your sales process.

**A worked example.** Say the problem is *"overwhelmed with content consistency."* Four solution pieces might be: (1) a content map so they finally know what to post; (2) your top five content tools; (3) how your unique VA approach takes content off your plate; (4) how repurposing saves you ~30 hours a week. Each one stands alone as content, each proves you know your stuff, and each bridges naturally toward how you help.

## **How Do You Turn One Idea Into a Month of Content?**

Take each solution and repurpose it across every medium, keeping the same core message.

One solution — say, your content-map approach — becomes a YouTube video, then micro-content for Instagram, TikTok, and Shorts, a podcast episode, a carousel, and a blog post. Same solution messaging, different formats. Then layer in **proof**: case studies, testimonials, "here's what this did for me and my clients." That's the step people skip — they jump straight from "here's your problem" to "buy my stuff." Don't. Bridge it with proof, because proof is what every buyer is actually looking for. When someone believes a solution will deliver the transformation they want, they'll pay what it's worth. When they don't, they default to the cheapest option.

## **Does This Work With a Small Audience?**

Yes — structure beats size. The goal is the *right* audience and a strong ratio of leads and sales to views, not vanity metrics.

We've watched this play out: one client's carousel with about 16 likes produced six applications. That's a ridiculous lead-to-engagement ratio, and it happened because the content was mapped with intention, not thrown into the void. People buy with emotion and validate with logic — that psychology doesn't change when platforms do. And when your messaging is dialed in and repeated, the algorithms understand who you're for and start serving the right people. So don't aim to be the "content class clown" chasing attention. Aim to be the right audience's obvious choice.
