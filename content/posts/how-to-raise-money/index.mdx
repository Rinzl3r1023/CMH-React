## **Why On Earth Would You Start a Business Against Titans of Industry?**

What impressed me about Renee was not her technical savvy, the wonderful features she's bringing to the audio and podcast community or the $13MM she recently raised to continue growth and expansion.

While I certainly don't aim to diminish those achievements, what defines Renee's success in my mind is in her ability to recognize an opportunity and seize it, regardless of circumstances and competition in her vertical.

Seriously, let me ask you a question...

Would you leave your Google job to go compete with Apple/iTunes?

Oh, and by the way, with no prior business experience in entrepreneurship. What a kicker, amirite?

But here's the thing.

Renee was 10000% right to do what she did.

As a habitual user of audio content, she recognized what most of us have known for some time but have yet to act on it; these podcast players are just that -- PLAYERS.

There's no unique experience.

No customize-able options like a Netflix that aims to curtail their content to satisfy the user while simultaneously helping movies and television series get discovered.

From a podcast host standpoint, this frustration is what drives people into "podfade" oblivion. Literally.

Did you know the majority of podcasts submitted to iTunes never make it passed 20 episodes?

Now, you'd probably never know that because the shows you usually discover have an established audience and are probably hosted by a name you recognize and admire.

But unless you somehow made New and Noteworthy or the What's Hot tab on iTunes, your chances of organic discovery are minimal, to say the least.

For us over here with my team, the New and Noteworthy wave is the only reason we're still here 130 episodes later and have had amazing guests from Shark Tank, NYT's Best Selling Authors and Primetime TV Hosts.

Without that, there comes a point in which everyone's pain threshold has been breached and it no longer makes sense to keep talking into a microphone with seemingly nobody listening on the other end.

Moreover, many podcast listeners are lurkers because they're on the move.

Quite literally.

Our data has shown the majority of our listeners consume content while they are commuting to work, at the gym, walking the dog, doing laundry, cleaning the kitchen...you get the point.

In other words, getting people engaged with audio beyond casual listeners is extremely difficult.

However, the audio format has been around since the advent of the radio.

So how can we take this new awesome creature that is digital, audio content and create something new and exciting that does it justice and not just try to cram it in an old shoe box that is the radio format?

Well, herein lies CastBox and people like Renee Wang.

Setting sail to save the audio world on both ends of the spectrum.

## **Why Does it Matter?**

This is my opinion and one that many may not share but I'd be remiss if I didn't clue you in on it.

One of the most powerful aspects of entrepreneurship is we have the ability to cause disruption and really move the needle forward to improve the human experience in more meaningful ways than anyone else in the world.

Quite the statement, wouldn't you say?

Here's why...

Let's say Renee makes so much noise and causes so much disruption in the audio vertical with which she operates in that she starts to challenge the ways things have been done before, all the way to the top of the food chain.

In the podcasting arena, that would mean Apple with the iTunes/Apple Podcast platform.

Apple, taking note of this disruption and sound waves Renee and her team have been making at CastBox, they decide it's in their best interest to either adopt her practices or acquire her, outright!

Guess what happens?

Renee just changed an entire industry, all by her lonesome.

You see, we tend to point at Corporate America and expect them to be better, do better and provide more but in reality, it's very rare to find these massive organizations as trendsetters.

The ones that do it best, sure!

But I would say the vast majority are too big to really turn the sails fast enough to have an environment of innovation and change.

Truth be told, that would be a pretty chaotic environment.

For example, look at who we're talking about here in this blog.

Apple...As in Steve Jobs, Apple.

The company that brought you the array of "i" everything and changed the way you use a mobile phone.

Yet, in many ways, Apple has struggled in other areas...Including the podcast app.

Same for Google.

Name one Google program that has thrived outside of being the world's biggest search engine?

Google Glass -- Flop

Google Fiber -- Meh, not looking so hot

Google Plus (aka the NEXT Facebook)!? -- Epic FAIL

But, what HAS Google done well?

Acquisitions.

Ever heard of YouTube?

Guess what mega-player owns that one?

Yep, GOOGLE.

**Go Google (pun intended) Google Acquisitions and you'll get a list about 45 miles long.

So, WHY does it matter that Renee has been bold enough to go to bat against these colossal monsters of business?

Because change starts with the little guys with big ideas and the maneuverability to pivot and find what users truly want.

Hedge your bet just right and you may find yourself among the stars, getting acquired for billions.

## **Don't Worry About Getting Lapped, You Don't Swim in the Same Pool**

If I hear one more entrepreneur do a quick search online and see someone doing their "idea" and that being the determining factor as to whether or not they decide to pursue the venture, I'm literally going to throw a six year old's fit right in their face.

My immediate response usually goes something like this..."And?"

The conversation usually turns into, "well, there's no way I could compete with them. They have an entire team, investment money. They are one of the biggest companies in the world!"

AND!!??

Here's the problem with our viewpoint on that matter.

We look at competition in the truest sense of the world as it relates to us.

Meaning, from a scarcity standpoint.

In high school when you tried out for the Varsity Baseball or Softball team, it was literally you or someone else that was making the cut. End of Story.

We got to a job interview and it's either you or one of the umpteenth candidates they have that applied for the position.

Slow, I get your perception has been a bit tainted and I forgive you for making a direct translation now that you're in the entrepreneurial world.

As of 2016, there are 7.442 billion people on this planet.

You know how much just 1 billion is?

Let's put it into something we can better conceptualize.

1 BILLION seconds is a whopping 32 years. My exact age as I write this post.

Okay, wow! Cool, Chris...

Wait, I'm not done!

How about 1 million seconds?

Answer: just under 12 days...

So, the difference between ONE million and ONE BILLION is quite literally more than astronomical. Only 12 days out of the 32 years!

Madness, right?

So, let's go back to our example.

there aren't just 1 billion but almost 7.5 billion people on the Earth.

Your job is to create enough value that just a few hundred of them care to pay you money for it.

When you look at it that way, it doesn't just change the lens you view it through, it throws out the whole damn picture.

And that's what I want you to be focused on.

Abundance, because that's now the world we live in.

Ready to take action?

You should be...
