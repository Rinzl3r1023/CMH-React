> ## **"Being a Woman in Business has Been My Greatest Challenge"**

It pains me to hear those words, especially from someone I both respect and admire.

When I moved to Austin some seven months ago, this Barbary Brunner lady kept popping up on my radar.

I heard nothing but the utmost respect for her work, her connections and for her as a person.

And so to have her on my podcast and here the challenges she's faced, as a female business person and entrepreneur, it's severely disheartening.

But it's also encouraging.

Because I've seen her in action.

I've seen how she responds when someone walks into a room five minutes late and she politely but very sternly & professionally rips them a new one.

And so I know she is fighting the good fight for other women that follow along the same path.

You know, the ones that feel they are alone.

But as I listened to her story and as we've moved through this Female Entrepreneur Series, I began to recognize something.

And it was that I was part of the problem.

But just for reasons you probably wouldn't immediately expect...

## **A Moment of Epiphany**

We sat on a deck overlooking Lake Washington in Medina, WA, just outside of Bellevue, and a very acclaimed entrepreneur, whom shall remain nameless, began speaking with my wife and I.

You see, we were there for what's called a business Intensive...

It's basically where you pay an exhorbitant amount of money to go work, hands on, with some thought leaders in your space.

In this particular case, these folks we were going to work with are arguably Top 5 in the world at what they do.

Needless to say, we were excited to be there and with such a small amount of customers attending (six) we knew we'd get hours of hands on guidance.

But the fact is, we were there for my wife's business, not mine.

We largely support one another and quite often do collaborative work but our businesses are entirely different.

Back to the deck convo...

This entrepreneur began speaking to us and was clearly focused on talking to me and viewing my wife as an afterthought.

He likely assumed something along the lines of this girl must just be his assistant to take notes...

And maybe I'm dead wrong.

But it certainly felt that way.

He wasn't rude...or ugly...or malicious.

But he clearly made a predetermined observation that I was the one to speak with about why we were attending.

When I said I was there in a supportive capacity for my wife's business, the look on his face confirmed my thinking.

Both surprised and taken aback, he struggled to find his next words.

And what's worse is I noticed throughout the course of that Intensive he struggled to NOT make what I felt to be borderline sexist comments.

Just as an example...

He painted this picture of what you have to be as a public figure selling products on the internet.

He gave a metaphor which he explained as the Tile and the Grout.

He then went on to elaborate what he meant by that...

Essentially, there are people on the internet that just make a lot of noise.

They do a wonderful job at marketing themselves but they're not necessarily a thought leader or the "expert" in that field.

These "noise makers" he refers to as Tile.

But when people buy programs from the Tile People, eventually they want MORE and will seek out the Grout.

The Grout are people like him (as he alluded to frequently), that don't have the visibility but far more skilled expertise.

Now, nothing wrong with that analysis...I would largely agree.

What bothered me and still rubs me the wrong way today is he implied that my wife was in the Tile People category.

Keep in mind, he's met this person less than 24 hours ago at this point and has no prior knowledge of her business.

But since she's a young, beautiful, woman, surely she must just be a Tile Person.

I can only assume it would rock his ego too much to know someone half his age AND female is not only far more visible on the internet but also knows her ish like very few in her industry.

She's been featured in Huffington Post, collaborated with SamCart, SEM Rush and now mentored by some of the biggest names in her space.

This is not my opinion but a fact...she's a rising star in her space and I'm constantly on the grind to match both her energy and brilliance.

I've never met ANYONE more dedicated to their trade and mastery of the source material by which they train and coach their clients.

All the while, she's got a YouTube channel that grows by the minute and built her online membership site to multiple six figures in less than 12 months all while helping me recover from a potential deadly and chronic health ailment.

So, while I licked my wounds and battled for my life to get my health back, this woman took the ball and ran so far her competitors will soon not even be close enough to sniff her dust, let alone eat it.

And so when this dude that's never met her and wouldn't know her from the man on the moon starts making bullshit comments and inferences based solely on the fact she's a female, him and I have big problems.

And before you write me off as just an angry husband, let's explore this just a little further...

## **The Internet is a Scary Place...Especially if You're a Female**

"We've got to start blocking certain regions of the world," my wife says with a disgusted look on her face.

"I'm tired of seeing pictures of men's junk."

Even her Assistant, Pearl, had gotten to a breaking point and was desperately searching for solutions.

It's also rare I don't notice my wife's phone light up on the counter top in our kitchen without seeing a random "Hey Baby" pop up on her Messenger App for her business page.

A quick scroll through any of her YouTube videos or Facebook comments on her Public Page will paint you quite the picture.

What's sad is if you see one of those comments it means it wasn't bad enough to be deleted.

Her team works daily to delete and scrub filth from all of the aforementioned platforms.

These comments have nothing to do with her content.

It's almost as if they saw three seconds of her video or took a quick glance at her picture and decided they had the right to slide in a d*ck pic or think that by some weird chance she'd be swooped right off her feet with a "Hey Baby" comment.

What's actually funny is when other fans, followers and subscribers chime in and defend her honor.

That, or they call them out and ask if they overlooked the big diamond ring on her finger.

But the reason I mention this is I rarely have the same problem...

If ever.

My podcast has millions of downloads and has gotten global visibility in 130+ countries.

We've had guests from Shark Tank, other Primetime TV hosts, New York Times Best Selling Authors and some of the most recognizable entrepreneurs in the world.

What's more is just a few short months ago my website experienced a 6,000% increase in traffic...and in just one week!

My inbox flooded with messages and my social accounts certainly picked up in activity.

But to my knowledge, very few "Hey Baby" type comments or genitalia photo shoots came through.

Now, it's possible and likely I'm just not as attractive as my wife.

But when my account or inbox does experience an issue, it's with the male variety as well.

Just the male variety that prefers other male varieties...catch my drift?

Conversation for another day.

I suppose what I'm getting at here is the internet is an amazing place where we can instantly connect and share with anyone on the planet.

It's also a place where the scum of the universe come out from under their respective rocks.

And the problem therein lies in a deeper rooted notion that isn't just skin deep.

Questions like how many people actually think and feel this way begin to emerge.

Is it just localized to a select few or do the many secretly hold these same values?

And so just as women were once afraid to travel alone (many still are) they are now just as, if not more, concerned about their safety in our digital landscape.

We've received threats against us, our families and have had to go to great lengths to protect our privacy.

Is that really the world we want to create and live in?

## **Where Does it End and How Can We Make a Change**

As I see it, and this isn't just isolated to the internet (by the way) it's on us as a collective to stand up when we see something wrong.

If I could go back to Washington State and tell that well-known entrepreneur to go stick it, I would have...

But I didn't.

And now he doesn't even know that what he did was wrong and potentially hurtful.

I'll always regret that.

But how many times have you scrolled through and saw some idiocy online, didn't click "Report Comment" and just went about your day?

When these people are held accountable in accordance with the varying degrees of their comments and actions, they either start a debate about why their viewpoint is valid and acceptable or they disappear.

I don't expect you to wage war with every heinousness you see online or in person.

But as a society, if we take appropriate action instead of turning our heads the other way because it doesn't "apply" to us, we fail to make a change.

Maybe that next Reported Comment is the one that gets this person booted from the platform.

Sure, they'll probably be back and with a different Egg Account but how much did you have to go out of your way to Flag or Report that comment?

Two clicks? If that...

And for those in-person opportunities, the ones where people inadvertently or maybe even intentionally demonstrate a chauvinistic mentality, my general rule is to address it but to do so with grace.

Maybe I say it somewhat playfully but still stern.

Something like, "Wait, so since she's a female she can't also be an expert?"

Or, less combative...

"What makes you think or say that?"

It's my guess you'll see the vehicle thrown into reverse and a recognition of their flawed narrative and thought process.

But to do nothing, as I did, is a missed golden opportunity that I assure you I will personally not miss again.

## **Going Forward**

As I write this I just got off the horn with Barbara Corcoran, real estate mogul and co-host of ABC's Shark Tank.

I proposed a similar question to her as it relates to the future of women in the workplace.

Her perspective was vastly different than what I expected.

To largely paraphrase, she said it's all about attitude.

She said she doesn't care about your race, sex, etc but just what you bring to the table.

She also believes the world is transitioning into a more equally competitive landscape where those things matter less and results are everything.

In her time with me, she also spoke of a time when she hiked her skirt up further, put on even more makeup and used her femininity to her advantage.

She didn't just earn the respect of her male counterparts, she demanded it...

But not by choosing to wage a war she probably recognized she couldn't/wouldn't win but by beating them at their own game but by doing it in HER way, not theirs.

To be totally candid, I feel torn about these various approaches.

Let me explain.

Do we take an activist approach or let the system be what it may and just win the game anyways?

And maybe it's not THAT simple.

In fact, I'm certain it's not.

And that's not to say anyone's right, wrong or indifferent in their own strategy.

But maybe in seeking an answer we've discovered our own.

Maybe we all have a unique way of approaching this particular challenge but it's an individualized one and not a universal, one size fits all, solution.

So where one person may be wanting, willing and able to take demonstrative activism by the horn and call out every form of malice they see, others may find beating the snot out of their male counterparts and competition to be far more suited to their style.

Barbara Corcoran is a global icon at this point.

She commands damn near anything she wants.

And my guess is she's not sitting on Facebook Flagging and Reporting trolls.

Meanwhile, there are Female Executives at organizations that wouldn't be where they are if they hadn't bulldozed their way into it, fighting the good fight and being very vocal about their activism.

Silence and just pure action in doing the job may not work in that respect.

I see many friends that struggle with this mightily.

Many of my minority friends are trying to figure out how and in what manner they aim to stand up and defend their civil rights.

And so I see them flailing.

Making a bunch of noise on Social Media but not getting anywhere in their professional life.

Not for lack of talent or ambition but for lack of focus.

Who am I to judge their choice in action, it's just a candid perspective.

So, here we are, pretty much back where we started.

Where do we go?

What's the best practice to demonstrate?

You should see what I actually drafted up to post...

It was like some sort of War Strategy.

And while I think it's all viable and certainly actions I intend to implement, I think it's more important to break it down into simplicity.

Things that stick, things people remember that cause them to take great action, are punctual but profound.

Look at our recent Presidents...

Obama built an entire campaign around one word: Hope

Trump built his entire campaign around one simple phrase: Make America Great Again (or MAGA)

These things tend to not only go viral but rile up a base to feel inspired.

So I'll aim to take a page out of their playbook:

It All Starts with Awareness

You see, the reason I didn't take action in Medina, Washington was not because I'm not bold enough.

It's because it was only until after the fact I fully contextualized what had happened.

You all may not have a wife that's a public figure...I do.

We both choose to live our lives in the Public Eye and were it not for that, I probably wouldn't have the awareness to share this message with you.

It's the AWARENESS I now have that inspires me to take action.

When we see these comments on YouTube or we hear some backhanded remark in a business meeting, we lack the awareness to recognize it for what it is.

What do the kids say these days?

Stay WOKE!?

And they're not wrong, as funny as how that's usually applied.

People criticize the Millennial Generation for being "offended" by everything but in reality what they're (and we, I'm a part of this tribe) are doing is unraveling centuries worth of conditioning and prejudiced viewpoints.

All of these start with your awareness.

The documentary you watched about the food you were eating.

Those campaigns have forced the entire fast food industry to change their tune.

Tyson Chicken got blasted in a viral video that depicted severe animal cruelty.

They immediately shifted gears.

We have this amazingly powerful tool and these larger than life voices in 2018.

And I trust you'll use them both gracefully and with candor.

Especially now that you're more aware.

Be well, friends...
