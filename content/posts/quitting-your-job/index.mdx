> ## **You Either Clean the Building or You Have Your Name On it**

Nicole Walters, whom will always remain ScOprah in my heart, blew through the gates, not just out of them.

But she had been conditioned from a very young age that you have very limited choices in this world.

Neither of her parents were entrepreneurs.

They came to this country seeking to provide for their family.

Not to build a thriving startup.

And so despite her early acknowledgement that the 9 to 5 probably wasn't the route for her, she soon found herself in Corporate America largely because of their guiding influence.

But what I love most about her incredible story is just how fast and seamlessly she was able to leave said job and not just launch a business but grow it to 7 figures and beyond!

Not only does this show you how possible it is in today's landscape but it ALSO dispels this notion that "it's too late" to become an entrepreneur.

Or that you've "wasted so much time."

But here's the thing...

## **You're Alw****ays Moving Forward, Even When You Don't Know It**

We've talked about this at length in a previous installment.

People who aspire to do their "own thing" believing they are stuck because they aren't yet entrepreneurs.

If you recall or if you're just now joining us (welcome), I rebuked that thought on one major premise...

To summarize, it went something like this:

> If you're entrepreneurial, truly entrepreneurial, it's highly unlikely you haven't been involved in an entrepreneurial endeavor by the time you've reached adulthood. This could be as simple as mowing lawns when you were younger, getting amped about the neighborhood garage sale and semi-running it with your mom and dad or really anything that falls outside of the traditional 9-5 format.

The point I was trying to make is that we don't just wake up one day, quit our jobs and voila, we're entrepreneurs.

And that's not to say others living under a rock haven't had this moment of epiphany because I do think we reach a point of crescendo.

I'll give you an example.

My wife, whom did not consider herself entrepreneurial at all, told me about her summer job as a mid-teen doing door-to-door Cutco sales in Puerto Rico.

Immediately, I had to interject and dispute the notion she thought she was NEVER an entrepreneur prior to becoming an entrepreneur.

She now runs a multi-six figure online business that's rapidly growing by the minute.

But here's the thing...

It's just the way our brains work if you are that way...

Conditioning, parenting, friends and structured academia may have pushed you in a different direction but I believe entrepreneurs were/are always innately entrepreneurs.

So, how does that apply to quitting your job and FINALLY making that transition into your first venture?

A large part of what I do in coaching actually starts well before the entrepreneur endeavor.

Isn't that wild?

High Level Executives, people with astounding degrees and possibly even Doctorates in their respective fields.

All of whom tell me how much time they've wasted.

Wait, what!?

And I get where this thought originates.

Just the other day one of my former students said she started her business because she got tired of making someone else's dreams come true.

Heavy, right?

But to think that's purely wasted time is such a misnomer.

And truthfully, it's largely what's preventing you, yes you too, from moving forward and having a thriving enterprise of your own.

You haven't wasted time, you've been building your book of business...just in different ways.

You've been establishing the key contacts you'll need to have a viable network.

You're establishing yourself as an authority in whatever it is you do.

You're learning how to effectively communicate in a business setting.

Learning the importance of how to work with people and show leadership qualities.

If you don't think ANY of those skills will apply to your business, we've gotta have a nice little sit down, my friend.

In fact, most of my clients that DO have experience in the work force are sometimes easier to work with and have much faster results than my little whippersnapper children.

Not actual children, just recent grads trying to build the next Facebook and swearing their idea is worth billions when they haven't even built a basic Wordpress site yet (I still love you too, don't worry, but I'm not signing your stupid NDA still).

In fact, in life I don't think we're ever *not* moving forward.

If you're breathing, and hopefully you still are, it's likely you're at least growing in some capacity.

It might not be as much as others or as much as you'd like but you have to really make a concerted effort to just completely not grow at all.

And so we have something to work with if you're willing to make the commitment to shift gears and adopt a new mindset.

All those skills and experience I referenced may not completely translate to the entrepreneurial space but we can easily convert that Word Doc into a PDF...Unless you're my mom, and then I'm going to be getting a call every time you need that done. (kidding, Mother!)

Now that we got that out of the way and we know we're not indeed stuck or have wasted a bunch of time, let's talk about the part that IS likely going to get in your way.

## **Making the Mental Shift is EVERYTHING**

People are so friggin smart and talented.

So smart that building a six figure business is NOT hard.

Let me repeat...

Building a six figure business is ***NOT***hard.

I've done it several times and I've helped many clients do it in six months or less.

You want to know what the majority of that work entails and what usually dictates how fast this transformation can happen?

That stuff between your hairy ears (Your's may or may not be hairy, I don't know).

Just like we tell ourselves we're so far behind or wasted so much time, etc, etc, etc...we also just can't get out of our own darn way when it comes to running our own business.

We think people won't be willing to pay for what we're offering.

"If such and such corporation is charging "X," then I can't justify offering this service for money," we exclaim!

Or, "there's already SO many people doing this, and some of these resources are already online for FREE!"

Or how about this one..."I'm really just putting feelers out there to see if this is something people would be interested in."

Three years of pushing out free content and we're still in the "feelers" mode.

But you don't get it, Chris!

I have a good job and real responsibilities like bills and kids and, and, and, and...

Get it, hear you...loud and clear (as always).

And there most certainly is a strategy for that...I'm getting there.

But I'm telling you right now you're biggest challenge is going to be embracing the entrepreneurial lifestyle.

The humility to put yourself out there and do the roll-up-your sleeves work to figure this thing out.

The months of persistently validating your idea and really refining it with a group of BETA users.

The constant recognition that you are indeed worth it and people need your solution.

Training your brain to think about unique ways to take this idea of yours and turn it into a product or service.

Ultimately, your ability to think like an entrepreneur.

To see what other people don't see and to learn how to craft an execution plan to get it done.

That very short list I just gave you, I hope you didn't think you could print this and check those off thinking that was it.

That list isn't even sniffing, let alone scratching, the surface.

And doing all of these things in some sort of meaning and effective order...forget about it, the overwhelm takes over.

My brother-in-law recently left his job and started his own Freelance Web Development business.

At the time I'm writing this, it's been seven months since he left his job and five months since he started his business.

Last month he had his first $6,000 month and has steadily been increasing his revenues month over month.

But do you know what's been the hardest part in coaching him?

*Which, by the way, he's an ideal client for me because he's amazingly coachable.

It wasn't the skill or the trade.

In fact, we have literally never one single time talked about anything development related.

We haven't talked code, design, CSS, XML, HTML...NOTHING, not a once.

But what we often talk about is his mindset.

How to get over the fear of cold pitching someone.

How to know when it's the right time to follow up with a potential client when you can tell they're ready to sign on the dotted line and to do so with confidence.

How to get over the fear of not being an extrovert but needing to put yourself out there and attend networking events.

Recognizing that just because there are 4,000 other developers (exaggerated, not an actual figure) and agencies in Austin, TX (HUGE tech & startup ecosystem) that there's still enough opportunity for him to make money.

And the first people that gave him negative feedback...he wanted to quit and do something else.

He was literally looking at Amazon FBA.

At his previous job, this guy built an entire CRM/Inventory Management system for an entire chain of Hospice Facilities...FROM SCRATCH.

I can't begin to tell you how challenging that is to do...

And yet, here's the same person, now in his own business and questioning whether his value is enough to do a basic 4-page Wordpress site for a Yoga Instructor.

Sounds crazy on paper but this is what we all go through...and then some.

And truth be told, the bigger and badder you get, the more some of those voices scream in your head.

So, what do we do?

We talk ourselves out of our own thing and utilize logic and reasoning to justify the decision.

That or we blame it on the timing.

Years go by and we're still at the same job...miserable and quietly wondering what-if.

## **Don't Just Jump...Maybe (The Art of the Side Hustlers)**

I jumped from the cliff and never looked back.

Broker than broke with literally not enough to even pay attention, as Dave Ramsey would say.

But I was 24 with no responsibilities other than my dog.

My college side hustle had grown and I was just fed up.

I knew I never should have taken a "real" job and not 18 months later I was kicking myself for doing so.

I operate in extremes and it's sometimes painful but it works for me.

I've learned to channel it into positives and not fully expose myself to a point I'm liable to get burned.

That has been through much trial and error, I assure you.

But I think some of us do need a bit of that mentality.

The Cortes burning of the ships approach.

Mentally, if not literally.

To just know you want something and to bloody freakin go for it!

And for those people, I recommend making sure you have your safety net in place.

Uber is a great safety net.

Renting out your second bedroom or sleeper sofa on Airbnb is a great safety net.

Refereeing basketball games on weekends is a great safety net.

For my young folks, I love this strategy because it does allow you to build your business full-time and put at least some kind of food on your table.

You're basically still a college kid and that's the budget you better plan to live on.

I lived LOWER than a college kid my first full year in business.

My little studio apartment was smaller than 400 sq ft. and right around $500/month.

Let's do some quick math...

Assume you can drive for two hours per day doing Uber.

And let's say you can drive 4-5 people in that duration of time (probably a conservative figure).

For shear numbers, I'll assume each ride nets you $8-12 dollars when you include tips (Again, probably conservative).

We're looking at around 14 hours worth of work and ~$315/week...or ~$1,260/month.

That would have given me almost $800 in living expenses after rent to live on.

In other words, more than doable.

Now, I see you NY, CA and DC folks...and your situation may be different.

By my estimate, even if you ONLY plan to work 40 hours/week TOTAL that still gives you 26 hours to work solely on your business. And all of that is assuming you ONLY work 40 hours/week!

That's an astronomical amount of time and I've seen people build wildly successful companies with just that amount of time if they're hyper-focused.

Bottom line is if you're going to leap and go for it, I really recommend having a plan to fall back on.

I got myself in big trouble by not doing this and had to really make it happen to not be forced to close my business.

It's not fun, super stressful and unnecessary.

Making the transition is a learning process and you need to give yourself adequate time to go through those growing pains.

## **Night and Weekend Warriors**

This generally applies more to people with heavy responsibilities.

Mom's with full time jobs that are high paying.

People that live in very expensive regions of the world (Looking at you again NY, CA, and DC).

These people can't live on a meager $1,260/month.

And I wouldn't even begin to ask them to do so.

But what about cutting your silly Netflix habits to build your future.

The Kardashians will (probably) still be on TV in two years...you can catch up and binge watch then.

The average person spends 45 bloody minutes on Facebook every single day.

And guess what, all these apps are working every single day to further engage you more and more with their platform.

So, bear with me here, but what if you could eliminate that nonsense time and focus on your business?

And I'm not talking about slaving away into the wee hours of the night.

I'm talking maybe 1-2 hours from 7-9 PM or maybe right after work from 5-7 before dinner or even while you're making dinner.

Is it ideal?

Of course not.

Will if be fun?

Again, probably not.

But will it be worth it?

You bet your ass it will be.

I live by no one's accord but my own.

I get paid to talk to my business heroes and write about what I love...

I have a new business or investment opportunity drop in my inbox literally every single day.

In the last six months alone, I've had about eight amazing opportunities to join up with an emerging tech startup or lead an Accelerator Program or get paid to do something really freaking cool.

I'm not telling you this to brag...I'm telling you because I paid my freakin dues.

I busted my ass when my friends were out having fun.

I sacrificed stuff I wanted to do because I wanted to live a life I loved and not one I tolerated.

You have no clue, and you never will, how much I want that for everyone.

There's too many unhappy people in the world without a clue as to what to do about it.

And meanwhile, you've got people that have "figured it out," raking in the cash and living what I call an Instagram life...you know, where they're always smiling and visiting new and exciting places.

Is that worth giving up your Kardashian fix?

That's a no-brainer to me.

Now, it's going to take time.

1-2 hours/day is a slow build.

But it is what it is, this is your situation.

Move to a cheaper city if you can or live on your spouse's income by cutting your silly and probably unnecessary expenses.

But don't whine to me about your situation.

These are the cards you've dealt yourself and it's your responsibility to change them...no one else's.

*Please don't forget I still love you despite the tough talk.

Notice I say Night and Weekend WARRIOR, not worker, and it's for a reason.

Time to get tough and see what you're made of.

## **Have Something to Bloody Sell and Don't Get Ahead of Yourself**

Most early stage entrepreneurs aren't thinking about monetizing.

At least they aren't being intentional about it.

They're waiting for _____.

I wait for no man, woman, child or thing.

This not monetizing problem is particularly bad for my online, lifestyle folks like experts, coaches and content creators.

They don't see the true value in their work and they've been told you have to put out free content to build an audience.

You do...and that's exactly what you're reading right now.

But if you don't think I'm writing this to turn you into a customer you're kidding yourself.

And I hope you do, we'll crush it together.

But these pages are pixeled, meaning you'll probably soon see my ads online.

You've probably also noticed my opt-ins and free resources.

That puts you on my email list and I am going to try to sell you a product.

Why is that a bad thing?

Right?

We don't walk into Walmart and expect ish to be free!

Can you imagine that!?

Literally, people up in arms because Walmart tried to sell them something.

Damn Walmart and all their stuff they want to sell me!

And yet people get frustrated with pop-ups on websites and opt-in forms as well as advertisements.

And yes, I know some people can be obnoxious on their websites...but that doesn't apply to everyone and nor should you treat everyone equally.

The problem is not that it happens, I could care less, but it's what it teaches newbies to the space.

They go so far the other way with their "ninja strategies" that they indirectly tell people they're basically open for charity.

The other problem I see is they just truthfully don't have anything to sell.

And so what I highly recommend is not spending crazy amounts of time on building out some ridiculous product that's going to take months for you to build all while you're not generating money.

Not unless you're seeking massive funding (entirely different topic for another day) and even then I would still have SOMETHING!

So, if possible, offer your solution as a service first.

Let's say you can take on consultations and help people with the work you already do at your job.

I'll give you an example.

Assume you work in HR.

I assure you there's no shortage of entrepreneurs and startups that need your advice for good hiring practices and the works.

Charge them by the hour and just lend your expertise.

The content you put out should revolve around HR practices and then you tease a free resources which leads to you adding them to your email list and offering a complimentary session.

In the session, you tell them about your amazing experience, listen to their problem and say you're more than capable of walking them through this hurdle or putting them on the right track..

You tell them your rate and hopefully sign them.

Now, down the road, you may learn much about their needs and then scale your knowledge by offering a product.

The tripwire is still the same, you're just replacing the service offering with a product.

Content that leads to a sale...not just content for the sake of content.

Now, there's many other examples I could provide you with but you get the general drift.

My Doctor is probably one of the best real-world examples I've seen.

His YouTube channel drives so much traffic to his site he literally is not taking on any new patients.

Meanwhile, he's formulating products to sell so he can limit his client work and focus on just selling the product.

Having something, ANYTHING, to sell means you can pave the way to an end result and not just do things for the sake of doing them.

Hopefully your path is obvious, like my Freelance Web Developer Bro-In-Law, and there's no questions as to what you have to sell.

If you're lucky, it's also going to be something in high demand like development work.

If not and it's going to take months of development work and a ton of startup capital, focus on building an audience and an email list of potential buyers when you're product IS ready. This goes such a long way with investors I can't even begin to tell you.
