## **Are You REALLY Ready For Your Leap of Faith?**

While doing my own research for this installment, I went and Googled "Leap of Faith" just to see what would come up.

Here's what the dictionary gave me...

> An act of believing in or attempting something whose existence or outcome cannot be proved.

I don't know how you feel but that could literally apply to nearly every decision I make in my life and more importantly, in my business.

As an entrepreneur, we're promised nothing.

There's no guarantee we don't lose it all tomorrow.

And, for many non-entrepreneurs, that's not a far stretch from their own reality either.

You may feel "secure" in your job but downsizing could truthfully happen at any moment.

Were that to happen, was your initial decision to join said company not technically a leap of faith in and of itself?

The point I'm making is nothing is a sure fire thing and I don't believe it's healthy to live life purely by this definition.

But the greater question, and the one I aim to propose to you today, is HOW do we decipher which leaps of faith are worth pursuing and how do we properly take them?

Right?

We all WANT that amazing story...

The one where we took the big chance and turned those good fortunes into a legacy.

If not one where people remember us at scale then one that at least provides for our families and demonstrates strong guiding principles.

All of that said, what's our qualifier here?

Do these qualifiers vary contingent upon the various cultures and peoples of the world?

In other words, if a person in Asia were to take a chance and move to San Francisco to work for the next big tech company, is that considered as much of a leap of faith as the young cub that decides to launch their own startup company in their hometown but with no outside funding?

I could see viable arguments made on both sides of that spectrum.

Leaving home for a great job but being halfway around the world, having (probably) zero friends or contacts and (probably) barely speaking the language OR doing something incredibly risky and unproven with a high probability for failure but doing so with the safety net being you could always move back in with mom and dad.

I don't think there's an obvious or immediate answer.

That is, unless you're operating under your own set of bias.

Meaning, you've been conditioned to lean a certain way due to a wide array of variables.

Depending on what the startup entailed, I would probably say go the entrepreneur route.

Possibly higher upside and the ability to be your own boss.

Unlimited earning potential and even if you "fail" most entrepreneurs either leverage that amazing experience into a wonderful new venture or into a high paying job.

If the tech company doesn't work out, well, I suppose you're still in San Francisco and there's an abundance of new opportunities.

But it's also crazy expensive and given the possible lack on English comprehension and fierce competition out there, it may be hard to land a gig immediately or at least until the money dries up.

Here's the problem...

I'm an entrepreneur and am literally, categorically and unequivocally unemployable.

Hearing the words 9 to 5 make my skin crawl.

The thought of not owning my day gives me night tremors.

So, my perspective, is unfortunately but majorly biased.

But...somewhere along the way, I've answered my own question.

And that's all that truly matters.

## **To Each His or Her Own**

Don't try to be something you're not.

How many times did you hear that one growing up?

You may still hear that voice in your head from time to time.

I think this statement could not be more true in this discussion.

Like I just said, I answered my own question.

I would go the entrepreneurial route.

It's what aligns with my truth and where I envision my trajectory headed.

But that very well may not resonate with you or your dreams.

And that's OKAY!

I repeat, that is A-OKAY!

In my view, the entire premise behind this thing we call life is to pursue our hearts desires, where God or the Universe is leading us and to provide as much value as possible in the process.

When we don't have that clear and concise direction, it's my belief people feel inadequate, unfulfilled and demotivated.

Like the friend we all have that has all the potential in the world but still chooses to live in his parent's flip house or basement.

I have more of those types of friends than I'd care to admit.

And despite my efforts to push them in the right direction, it's subtly all wrapped around the direction I would personally take but wasn't authentically true to themselves.

It has to be true to you and who your are, your heart has to pull without you pushing in this direction and God or The Universe is opening up doors to naturally pave the way.

And that, as I see it, is how we determine how and even when it's time to take THE leap of faith among all of these minor leaps of faith we take every single day of our lives.

## **On the Ledge? Now What!?**

Before you close your laptop or minimize your screen and exclaim to your family that ole CMH told you to go do _____, let's cover some ground rules first.

I do this for your own benefit and for the benefit of my already flooded email inbox.

Jot this down, screen grab this page or download our free PDF to keep with you:

1. Hear out your loved ones

- When I say hear your loved ones, that doesn't mean interrupting them to continue your sales pitch on why this is such a great idea. Give yourself a rule that you're not going to interrupt ANYONE and allow their opinions to be heard. You should be taking notes of the objections, they're probably all super valid.
2. Look before you jump

- This one seems easy enough, right? There's a bigger point to be made here, though. When we're excited about a new opportunity, especially when unhappy, the brain doesn't rationally contextualize like it should. Meaning, you're extremely vulnerable to make a highly emotional decision. This is why people make very poor decisions after a divorce or tough breakup. Your emotional brain will beat out your rational brain 10 days a week (Yes, I know there are only 7 days in a week). It's critical you really give yourself time to stop and analyze your decision before you go for it. The longer you wait, and that doesn't mean forever, the less likely the emotional brain is going to prevail. I have a 72 hour rule, minimum, when exploring big decisions. And even that is a rapid turnaround. For life-changing decisions, I can sometimes take months.
3. Make sure your parachute is actually packed

- Seems like yet another no brainer, yea? You'd be shocked how many people just jump into a new opportunity without their metaphorical parachute. Things like how much money is saved up, how do you plan to eat, where are you going to live or how are you going to adjust your lifestyle to fit this new chapter? You have to prepare for the best but plan for the worst. Have you ever lived away from home? Ever worked in an environment where results are demanded and not on your own terms and in a town you're not familiar with? Fill in the gaps as you may or add your own questions, but actual preparedness and psychological preparedness are of the utmost importance here. Have an actual plan.
4. Set up your ground resources

- Once you've landed, how do you intend to give yourself the support you need? Maybe it's a new job and it's going to make evenings a massive challenge to put home cooked meals on the table. Maybe you just moved to an entirely new city and it's time to find social groups that fit your interests to combat the inevitable loneliness. If it's an entrepreneurial endeavor, you probably need to find some mentors ASAP! Give yourself the platform to succeed and thrive, it will limit your chances of going back and feeling like you made the wrong move.
5. Does this align with where you see yourself in 5 years...in 10 years, 20 YEARS!?

- This one blows my mind. People doing things that do not in any way align with who they are or what they want to be in the world. It's often in those cases they're doing it for the wrong reasons. Maybe it's parental pressures, often times money comes heavily into play or the other big one I see revolves around proving something to yourself and others (self-worth seekers). If this is something there's no way you see yourself doing forever, why would you do it now AND how much better served are you to move down the path towards something that DOES align with you but doing it now rather than later?

## **It's Not Black and White**

I can hear your objections now...

**It's not that simple, Chris!**

And I completely agree.

If it were, they wouldn't call these leaps of faith.

We'd all just be robots walking around viewing everything through a binary lens.

But that's not who we are as creatures.

We innovate, we create new and amazing things in the world.

We change course and completely reinvent ourselves.

Move to new cities to explore everything this planet has to offer.

And that's all amazingly exciting.

So, whether you're on the fence about your leap of faith and trying to convince yourself to take it or you're ready to roll the dice and buy your plane ticket, just know nothing is permanent.

As long as you've done your diligence, meditated and prayed over it and included the thoughts of your loved ones, there is no WRONG decision.

Whether it's a massive success or a decision that seems to land you right back where you started, you're always moving forward , even when it doesn't feel like it.

To error is human...

But to dare is greatness.

Be blessed.
