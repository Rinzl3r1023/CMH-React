## Supporting Black-Owned Business With Sterling Brown

Being an activist for personal cause and black lives matter, didn't interest Sterling as most people would have because he didn't see the big picture.

He said that he is very fortunate that the white people around him were great. Although the school experience that he had, he received some jokes about him not being black enough or not really black and attempts at humor, really it stereotypes. They make sense, but he admits that it hurts sometimes, but he had to toughen up and thicken up his skin, because the thing about jokes is that there's a little bit of truth to them, that's why they sting.

According to Sterling, if that continues to be the case throughout your upbringing into your young adulthood, your ability to pull together will be weakened.

It would be much better if we could have less division and more unity, which is a very contrived thing, just a platitude if you will. By being divided, it just increases these negative forces that are preventing us from moving forward. Right now, there is just too much division. There are too many things that we're arguing about when in reality, there's way more in common amongst all of us, all different colors, sexes, everything than there are differences. Unfortunately, we are always harping on those differences, and race tends to be a part of that, between the black and white communities and then within other communities.

Sterling emphasized that he never really took black activism probably the way that he should have. And not really identified himself as much of the black person that he is. And that was the result of white people making the joke, "*Oh, you're not really black.*" While black people were saying the same thing, "*you're not really black*." Those same people made hurtful comments, but at other times they put their arm around him, they would be supportive in different ways. But what those comments do is they tend to bring one down.

What black activism does now help builds you up. So as long as that black activism is very positive and bringing people together. A white person saying to a black person you're not really black, it's a double put down because a lot of times white people are viewing black people as being less. And when they say you're not even that. So you're less than less. It's a pretty offensive thing to do as a joke. But these little things over time, they do tear you down.

However, supporting black-owned businesses is something that Sterling is actually very proud of and fond of. He's trying to research and gather more information. So in his own way, he can help. He created a website and focused on reaching out to other black people and seeing if they can all work together and grow their businesses because growing their businesses is the best way to help out their family. Family is the first thing before helping friends, he said. Since black people tend to be more friends and their friends, tend to be black, so that's a great way to help the community while also helping the economy. So that's been a new focus of his.
