## **Why We Fail At Goal Setting**

I just started [Michael Hyatt's Full Focus Planner](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B0797FKZ7M/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=chrismharris1-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=B0797FKZ7M&linkId=0953c63aa55c8e07ba647786520a331a), which I highly recommend you look into!

In the weeks that have followed, I've had some of the more productive weeks I can remember.

And the thing is, I usually consider myself to be an extremely productive person.

But, here's the thing...

Goal setting is a daily science, not some pipe dream.

Often times, what we do is let the little drill sergeant in our heads just drive us crazy about what we NEED to do and what we're NOT doing but provides little-to-no detail on what needs to be done to accomplish that goal.

**For Example:**

Man, I'm barely going to make rent this month!

I really need to do a better job at managing my budget.

In fact, where did all that money go?

I mean, I know I went out with friends for dinner a few times but other than that...

Hmm, how do people afford to do all the things I see them do when I peruse Facebook News Feed?

They must just make way more money than I do!

Gosh, maybe I just need to make more money.

No! Surely I'm making enough!

I got a degree and this job was a huge accomplishment.

Remember, we had that big party when I was hired?

Okay, well maybe I'll just keep an eye on it and when I get a promotion in a few months it will be fine...

(End Story)

Sound like the thought process of someone that has a tangible plan for achieving their goal?

And I know your story may sound a little bit different, you get the jest.

Playing Monkey Mind just doesn't work...

And I have facts to back it up.

Did you know you are 40% MORE LIKELY to achieve your goals just by writing them down?

You read that correctly, I didn't add a zero.

A whopping FORTY PERCENT by just writing something on a piece of paper.

Now, let's get to *why* I think that's the case and what *you* can do about it.

## **We Don't Set Goals that Matter to Us**

When you REALLY want something, you tend to make it happen.

I know because I see some of the behavior on Black Friday.

Camping out in front of a Walmart in 20 degree weather just for a shot at a discounted television.

Madness...

But why can't we apply that type of commitment elsewhere?

Is it for lack of willpower and self-discipline?

Read that one again...

This is where we go astray.

We greatly overvalue willpower and self-discipline and while I'm not here to say they're NOT important, I am here to tell you it's not why you aren't accomplishing the goals you set for yourself.

The reason you stayed outside and froze half to death for that TV was because it mattered to you.

Like, REALLY mattered to you.

But what if we sat down and I took notes on what really matters to you in your life?

Have you even thought about that recently?

We hop on this merry-go-round called life and we just tend to ride the ups and downs waiting for it to come to a stop.

So, I'll ask you again...what matters to you?

Let's go back to our example of not having enough money for rent.

Let's say we establish a goal that says something like I want to save enough money to buy a house.

While we're at it, let's write down some reasons as to why this is important to you.

Maybe you want to feel empowered and like you're getting ahead in life.

Maybe you want to make your parents proud.

Perhaps you want to have a place that feels like your own.

You want a yard for your dog to play in...

Really get specific about why this matters to you on a deep and meaningful level.

Once we accomplish that, let's write down what realistically needs to happen to make this a reality.

Okay, we need to budget better because clearly that's not happening.

So, we're going to download Mint.com to create a personal budget.

Maybe you're on the other end of the spectrum and not making enough money.

Then the goal needs to be find a better job, a full time job or a side job to increase your earning potential.

I heard that Uber thing is a good way to make some extra money.

Hey look! They are a sponsor for my podcast :)

Now, and this is the critical part right here so pay attention...

We need to establish a finite date for when this goal needs to be accomplished.

Better yet, let's break it down even further.

How about you want to save just 1/4th of the money needed to put a deposit down on a house and you want to do that in six months.

The last thing I want you to do is write down a reward for yourself for achieving this goal.

Something that really will make you feel like you've earned it!

## **I Live and DIE By My Rituals**

I emphasize die because I literally sometimes feel worthless without my rituals.

Seriously, you should ask my wife.

She can tell when I've skipped steps.

And it's not just rituals in the morning.

It's rituals in the evening, at the end of my work day and even in between daily activities.

You see, we set the tone when we reset ourselves.

I talk about rituals quite often and it's for good reason.

And the beautiful thing is they can be different for everyone.

Now, if your ritual is to take a few shots of Vodka before the work day, that's probably not advisable, nor healthy.

So, let me clarify...healthy rituals.

Rituals that put you in the best frame of mind to take on the day.

Maybe you have some already, great!

I'll assume you don't and give you some of mine.

I usually start my day by eating a hearty breakfast...groundbreaking, I know.

But seriously, for me skipping breakfast just doesn't work.

I really operate well with a high protein diet. (Somewhere around 40-60g of protein, to be exact)

While I'm eating, I don't glare at my phone.

In fact, I have a rule to stay the hell away from my phone until my AM rituals are over.

Instead, I read a book.

That book is usually uniquely selected to match what it is I'm working on in my life at that moment.

This really inspires me to continue my work and gets my creative juices flowing.

Now, calm down...I don't go crazy and I don't expect you to either by reading 50 pages a day or some madness.

I aim to do 10-20 pages, depending on the nature of the reading material.

At that pace, I can knock a book out in about two weeks.

Next I do my 23 minutes of meditation.

I know it's 23 minutes because the one, specific guided meditation I do is 23 minutes. ([It's a Dr Dispenza meditation](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B072LRZ37S/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=chrismharris1-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=B072LRZ37S&linkId=1ef24740ef343f32268a37365a7529ee))

The meditation really gets my brain aligned with my body and gets me in a nice flow state.

From there, I do a quick workout and my stretches.

I do my shower, finish with 30 seconds of cold water.

I sit at my desk, glance at my planner to outline my day and get to work on the MIT (Most important things)

Michael Hyatt calls these the big 3. Either way, same premise.

## **It Doesn't End There**

I focus on my most important things to be done by noon and then focus on basic tasks in the afternoon.

These tasks can be deferred, if needed. (Obviously, pay your rent on time)

In between each of these blocks I've established for my day, I take a 15 minute break and do my breathing exercises.

The brain works better in sprints, not marathons.

When I end my day, I review my planner, check off what I completed and what I didn't get to.

I take a few notes on where I felt I excelled and what I hope to improve upon for tomorrow.

My focus is then on cleaning my desk area so I don't start the next day with a cluttered area to work.

I do the same at my home.

I do a quick disengage exercise to transition from work day to personal life.

And then before I go to bed I wrap up with an evening meditation (also 23 minutes) and go through all of my evening rituals before bed.

This might sound super overwhelming to you.

It did to me when I first implemented them but they've not just become what I do.

In fact, there's probably many things you do already that you don't recognize are habits.

Dare we check how many times you got on Facebook or how much time you spent there?

That right there is sometimes enough to do all of these rituals twice.

## **Dude, I Don't Want to Be a Robot**

This is usually where the conversation trends at this point when I talk to someone.

They don't always say it...

In fact, many times they don't.

But when I ask they immediately laugh so I know they were thinking it.

Here's my response to that.

Jocko Willink (Former Navy Seal and host of Jocko Podcast) says there's freedom in discipline.

And I know we boo booed on that discipline word earlier but here's where I want you to apply it.

Discipline only works if discipline is applied to attainable goals.

Hear me out again.

Discipline only works if it is applied towards attainable goals.

I'll give you an example.

You see these runners come to the finish line and although they've been dying for the entire race, possible have bloody toes and maybe even dehydrated, there eyes light up and they run through the finish line faster than they ran they entire race.

We get excited when we can see the end in sight.

When you are disciplined to get up and make yourself breakfast...no big deal.

When you're disciplined to read 10 pages...no big deal.

Disciplined to lay down and meditate for 23 minutes...done!

But when I say be more disciplined and stop spending so much money to buy a house...

Well, that's a bit more vague because the mind can't immediately visualize what that needs to look like.

## **We Win When We Learn to Win the Day**

The point is, none of these long-term goals we set for ourselves are going to happen overnight.

Willpower and discipline are finite resources.

So, in order to achieve our long-term goals, we must focus on taking just one step closer to them every single day.

In other words, you don't go from zero to BOOM overnight.

It's a long and arduous process...which is why we give up.

We achieve the long and arduous by setting ourselves up with short-term, daily wins while also keeping our eyes on the prize.

These subsets of your goals, like the steps required to achieve your goals, are what we need to focus on.

Again going back to our example.

I can't tell you how proud you're going to feel when you connect your Mint account to your bank account and see just one month where you stayed within your budget.

You'll truly feel like a millionaire, even if that only meant you put an extra $200 in the bank.

And this is where I always make sure to reiterate.

Consistency creates momentum which in turn equals motivation.

So often we reverse that equation.

We tell ourselves we're just not a motivated person and beat ourselves upside the head with that negative banter.

For lengths of periods of time it actually gives us short-term results and we further the lie.

The Drill Sargent in our heads gets us up and fired up through negative motivation but it's ALWAYS short lived.

And if you've somehow made that work for you, which I doubt, good for you!

For the rest of us normal, mere mortals, the science just doesn't back that up.
