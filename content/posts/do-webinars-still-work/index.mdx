## The Red Flags: Why Some Webinar Empires Are Crumbling

Look, there are some concerning trends out there.

I've watched creators who built *entire* businesses around webinars just... ghost them. Poof. Gone. Take Amy Porterfield's Webinars That Convert – that was her flagship for over a decade.

Sunsetted. Done. Not a great sign when a powerhouse like that pulls the plug.

But here's the caveat: The same folks saying "webinars are dead" are also claiming ads don't work anymore.

And I'm calling BS on that. I'm not talking small-time stuff here – I'm in masterminds with the big dogs, the guys who *coached me* back in the day.

They're posting lower CPAs than they've seen in five, six years. Better CPCs. Straight fire. So if ads are thriving for the pros, why are webinars tanking for some? It's not the format. It's *how* you're using it.

## The 2019 Webinar Trap: Hype Without the Goods

The old-school webinar? You know the one. "Stick around for 90 minutes – I've got a massive surprise at the end!"

It was all about building hype, teasing the dream life, and dropping just enough sizzle to get folks excited. Amy's stuff was solid, don't get me wrong – she delivered value.

But we've all sat through those duds where you walk away thinking, "Cool story, bro. But what did I actually *learn*?"

The problem? Those webinars were built for a *problem-aware* audience.

People craving the lifestyle, the quick win, the "aha" opportunity they hadn't heard before. It worked because it was fresh. But fast-forward to now: We're *solution-aware* AF. You've heard the pitch 500 times. "Join my course and transform your biz!" Rinse, repeat, refund.

Show rates? Trash. Stick rates? Non-existent. And that excitement? Fizzled out like a bad soda.

You can't run a 2019 webinar to a 2025 offer.

It's a double whammy – outdated messaging to an outdated product. Courses as we knew them? On life support. Unless they're hyper-specific, mechanism-driven transformations.

No more vague "become your best self" vibes.

## What *Does* Work: Flip the Script to Value That Pays Off

So, how do you make webinars (or workshops, masterclasses, challenges – whatever you call 'em) convert again? Simple: Make 'em *worth paying for*. Deliver real value upfront. Here's why and how:

1. **Market Awareness Shift**: Your people aren't problem-aware anymore. They're solution-aware and burned out. Hit 'em with specificity – *who* it's for (psychographics + demographics) and *what* it nails (tangible results). We've got a client running a three-week live launch – all trainings, no fluff. Two layers of specificity, actual results? It's crushing.
2. **Emotional + Logical Punch**: People buy on emotion, justify with logic. Keep the hype – get 'em fired up! – but back it with a blueprint. Not teases. Real mechanisms. Remember Ben Stein's Clear Eyes days? It doesn't have to be dry like PBS. Pump the energy, but make the logic ironclad.
3. **Do and Take Offers Rule**: Shoutout to Keith Krantz (genius Meta buyer, marketer's marketer – dude's a beast). He breaks offers into four types, borrowed from a client: **Build** (DIY info, like old courses), **Become** (self-transformation, à la 75 Hard), **Do** (with you – coaching, challenges), and **Take** (done-for-you, like a custom GPT or SaaS).

We're in a **Do** and **Take** era. People are exhausted from "build it yourself." They want centralization – one tool stack, not 75 apps.

(We're sponsoring GHL right now, not 'cause it's perfect, but 'cause it lets 'em *take* and implement fast.)

New webinars that win? They're "Do with me now, take the rest later." We do this in our trainings: Partial mechanism live, full access behind the coaching wall. AI-powered?

Heck yeah – we're building a suite of GPTs for sales pages, webinar scripts, all baked with psych principles. Modalities change; psychology doesn't.

Infomercials from Billy Mays? Cold calls? Snail mail? Same principles. Just new wrappers.

## Own Your Audience: Don't Rent from Big Tech

Quick detour – this ties in big time. Platforms are borrowed audience. Instagram changes the algo?

You're screwed. We had to nuke a Facebook page during COVID (restrictions suck). New ad account? Fresh start? Nah.

Our email list – tens of thousands after hygiene (we're talking hundreds pre-clean) – saved us. Email 'em: "New page, follow up!" Retarget, text, cross-pollinate.

Renting vs. owning. Build that list. Sender rep matters – no vanity metrics. Focus on openers/clickers. Borrowed audience vanishes overnight; owned is forever.

## The Playbook: Transform, Don't Inform

Bottom line: Ditch "webinar." Call it a workshop. Charge for it. Make it transformational – *do* part of it with them, *take* away tools.

AI flooded us with info (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude), so info's worthless now. Everyone's "rich" on paper. What they crave? **Answers**. Wading through the noise to *their* truth.

Layer on specificity: Who (your exact avatar) + What (hyper-focused outcome).

Give the aha – "This is possible *for you*, even after 500 fails." Map their pains: What they've tried, bombed at, still ache for. Your offer? The bridge to success.

Selling? Nail the psych: Value-stack the hell out of it. Bonuses so juicy they'd buy for those alone.

Counter objections head-on. Scarcity (buy now or miss out).

Anchor pricing (group vs. 1:1). I grew up in sales – even on my A-game, miss these? Conversions tank 50%. Charisma's cool, but principles close.

We ran a three-day workshop (Jeff Walker/Stu McLaren style). Post-2019, post-ChatGPT world. 30% conversion. *30%* paid yearly upfront – thousands per. Top-tier folks hit 10%. That's the power.

People still want human connection. Community. Live Zooms. Voxer access. Not "I'll hold you accountable" (that scares 'em – ego alert). Sell benefits; they'll self-sell the discipline. Like the military: "Serve your country, free education." You nod to the secondary wins.
