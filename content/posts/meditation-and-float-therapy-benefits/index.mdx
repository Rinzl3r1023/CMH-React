## **We All Have Our Issues, It's About How You Deal With Them**

When I met Edgar upon my arrival to True Rest Float Spa, I was new to town and trying to strike up meaningful conversations with anyone whom would take the time to do so.

A networker at heart, I recognize when you relocate to a new town, half-way across the country, it's imperative to get yourself plugged in with like-minded people.

Well, I certainly didn't expect one of my most substantial conversations to come with me in my Crocs waiting for my float session to begin.

More than an hour or so later, I recognized I was probably about to get Edgar in trouble with his work as he was indeed on the clock when our conversation began.

But I was impressed with his interest in a wide array of topics and his knowledge for someone at the ripe old age of 19.

Truth be told, my interests at his age revolved around the opposite sex and the next party.

Edgar, on the other hand, was mentioning books about the meaning of life, rituals and practices to enhance the human experience as well as strategies to present yourself in the best possible light upon a first impression.

He expressed his desire to one day start his own business and I knew my mentor tendencies were about to kick in.

You see, I spend ample time connecting with the younger, aspiring entrepreneurs because I recognize being an entrepreneur is quite literally the hardest thing I've done and continue to do in my life.

Without the proper guidance and resources, many will feel external pressures to give up on their calling and just aim to live out a life of what's been defined to them as normalcy.

When this happens, we strip the world of a unique resource.

When I learned of Edgar living in his car and/or couch surfing with friends, I was taken aback.

Not because I was judging him but because I knew it probably stemmed from some relational issue, more than likely with one or both of his parents.

I see this often with young entrepreneurs and that's why I wanted to address it and have Edgar come on the show.

Even coming from a business background, so to speak, my parents were vehemently concerned with my decision to launch my first venture straight out of college, despite our initial successes and proof of concept.

There's an evolutionary process that we all go through as entrepreneurs.

Not just one of figuring out how to make money but also adopting a completely new mindset, discovering who or what we're going to serve in the world and then emotionally reconciling how we're going to process the emotional ups-and-downs that are just part of the lives we lead.

I always say this but it's so valid...Entrepreneurship is a lifestyle, not a career.

Mostly everything you've learned to this point with have to be re-learned or, at the very least, significantly altered to prepare you for the arduous journey ahead.

## **The Science Behind The Ritual**

Neuroscience has now proven we can literally alter the way the brain is wired in just a few short days of meditation.

In fact, a recent Yale Study showed a decrease in the DMN (Default Mode Network) of the brain, also referred to as the Monkey Brain.

This brain network is largely responsible for mind-wandering and self-referential thoughts.

It's also been found to being associated with rumination, particularly over stressful events in the past or future.

Imagine, just for a second, how many times in the last week you've dealt with that and what your efforts would look like if you could dial that down a few notches?

Might serve dividends, am I right?

What's more, another research project at Harvard found that meditation and mindfulness training actually found an increase in cortical thickness in the hippocampus, which is largely responsible for learning and memory.

Try taking on your next major initiative with your learning ability and memory on it's A-Game and see if the results aren't overwhelming.

So, why don't we do it, you might ask?

In my opinion, and one point Edgar made I largely agree with, is we view a meditative ritual as a burden or a chore.

And I get it!

I meditate for 42 minutes on a daily basis.

Sometimes it's not always convenient and most days I have to plan my entire morning around doing it.

But here's the thing, for me it's now like skipping breakfast (I know the arguments around intermittent fasting, etc, but just as a figure of speech).

I now it's not just nutritious for my body but I also know how much I'm minimizing my potential output for the day.

In other words, why go through the day at a rate of reduced potential when in 42 minutes I can be at my peak?

Doesn't make much sense.

Doesn't mean I'm perfect and definitely doesn't mean I don't have days where I have to drag myself in the room to just knock it out.

Do your best, stay committed and the change your experience alone will be enough to keep you going, I assure you.
