I'm going to show you how I use [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com) to build months of content ideas in just a few minutes.

Quick heads-up first: this isn't a paid or sponsored post. I'm sharing a tool I actually use. I'd suggest YouTube as a content platform for almost any business right now — especially as a counter to what AI is doing to written SEO. But let's get into what vidIQ actually does for content ideas.

## **What Can You Do With vidIQ's Free Browser Extension?**

You get real value even before upgrading. The free extension shows trending videos, VPH (views per hour), competitor data, and keyword scores. Those scores combine search volume with competition level.

Here's how I use it. Say you search "Trello." vidIQ shows you the top channels for that term, plus related topics — "how to use Trello," "Trello tutorial," "Trello for project management." Each one gets a volume score and a competition score. On my wife Kim's channel, she ranks just behind Trello itself for that search term. That shows what this approach can build over time.

One warning: you'll want to chase anything scored "green." Don't treat that as a hard rule. I'll explain why next.

## **Does Every Video Need to Rank on Search?**

No. Some of your best content isn't built to pull in new viewers at all. Instead, it's built to go deeper with the audience you already have.

We talk about this with clients all the time: Attract content versus Connect content. **Attract** content gets you found. It targets search volume and reaches people who don't know you yet. **Connect** content builds trust with people who already follow you. A Connect piece may never rank in search. But it can show up as a suggested video, or land well when you share it straight to your subscribers. Either way, it pushes people further down your content funnel toward working with you.

So don't force every idea to be a high-volume Attract piece. If you do, your channel starts to look like a billboard instead of a relationship. Some pieces exist to make your audience feel understood. That's how you build know, like, and trust.

## **How Does vidIQ's Boost Feature Generate Content Ideas?**

vidIQ's paid Boost tier adds an auto-refreshing list of video ideas built around your channel. It banks months of saved ideas from one feature.

Once you've published some content, Boost starts suggesting topics based on what your channel already covers. You can dismiss whatever doesn't fit. For example, a generic "SWOT analysis" video made zero sense for a marketing-focused channel, so I dismissed it fast. Whatever you keep drops straight into your Saved Ideas tab. I still have ideas in mine from ten months ago. It banks up quickly.

As you sort through the suggestions, label each one by intent. Take "Overcoming emotional roadblocks in entrepreneurship." People don't actively search for that phrase. So it becomes a Connect piece — content that makes your audience feel understood, not content built to rank. On the other hand, "the one strategy you need for online sales" taps into real search behavior. That's an Attract piece.

## **How Do You Make Sure an Idea Actually Fits Your Brand?**

Run every suggested topic through an AI tool with your brand context before you commit to building it.

Here's the move: take a topic vidIQ recommends. Then feed it into [Grok](https://grok.com) or [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com) along with your brand positioning and who you serve. Ask it to shape the topic so it fits your specific audience — not just whatever's trending right now. Information alone is no longer the main value. Personal use and real outcomes matter more. So a topic with a "high probability of doing well on YouTube" only counts if it also speaks directly to your person.

## **Can vidIQ Help You Write the Actual Video Script?**

Yes — but some features work better than others.

vidIQ's AI "Create" tools can go from a topic to a usable outline or script in seconds. Feed in a title, and it builds a title option, an outline (hook, intro, problem, and so on, with a call to action), and even a draft script. In my experience, the outline-to-script flow is genuinely helpful. It can take you from a blank idea to something you can record almost right away.

That said, I'll be honest: the thumbnail generation isn't there yet. I'd treat that output as rough inspiration, then build the actual thumbnail in [Canva](https://www.canva.com).

## **Why Does Being a "Content Machine" Matter More Now?**

Because AI tools now handle both discovery and curation — not just discovery. So consistent, well-documented creators get surfaced more often.

With old-school search, Google gave you a list of links. You did the sorting yourself. Now, AI tools crawl everything and hand you one curated answer. That shifts the value toward being the creator an AI recognizes as a reliable source. My prediction — and this is purely speculation on my part — is that AI platforms may eventually pay creators a kind of contributor fee when their content gets pulled into an AI answer. Nothing confirms that yet. But building a strong content presence now seems smart either way.

Here's a real proof point: I used vidIQ's research tools to optimize Kim's entire YouTube channel in one afternoon. That same work would have taken months without it.
