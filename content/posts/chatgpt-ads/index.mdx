Let's talk about the recent announcement that ChatGPT is going to allow advertising on its platform.

I'll be honest — I had real questions about this move, because it bumps against the core promise of the platform. When you run [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com), you're not asking for a list of links to sort through; you're asking for *the answer*. Google ads only ever inserted a sponsored option into a discovery process *you* still curated. AI platforms promise discovery *plus* curation — so the moment paid placement enters the answer itself, people start to question whether the answer is the best one for them or just the one someone paid for. That tension is real, and worth naming up front.

But here's the flip side, and why I think this matters so much for you as a business owner: I believe ChatGPT ads could become the biggest advertising opportunity we've seen since the Facebook heyday. Let me walk through why — and to be clear, this is my prediction, and I'll hedge my bet on it. Let's get into it.

## **Why Is ChatGPT Adding Ads?**

Because OpenAI, like most fast-growing tech companies, is under pressure to build a real revenue model — and advertising is one of the most proven ways to do it.

It's the classic playbook: win massive adoption first, make yourself indispensable, then figure out monetization. We saw versions of it with early Uber and WeWork. I won't pretend to have OpenAI's exact financials (and I'd be skeptical of anyone throwing around precise numbers), but the direction is clear — building durable revenue is now essential for them, and ads are a natural lever once you've become a daily utility for millions of people.

## **How Do Google and Facebook Ads Actually Compare?**

The two giants of paid ads win in opposite ways — and understanding that is the key to why AI ads could be special.

**Google ads** are *intent-based*. Someone searches for exactly what they want, and your ad shows up against that intent. Google is also a true *utility* — nobody ever says "I need to stop spending so much time on Google." That means enormous, reliable volume. The catch: targeting is relatively broad (you're bidding on search terms), so it tends to be expensive. It's fantastic low-hanging fruit for high-intent searches.

**Facebook/Meta ads** are the opposite. They're *non-intent* — nobody logs into Facebook to buy a course — so you're doing pattern interrupt, which takes real marketing skill. But Meta's *detailed targeting* is what made it so powerful and, relatively speaking, cheap in its heyday: you could define exactly who your person was and reach them in narrow niches. The trade-off today is platform relevance — Facebook skews older now. If you sell to roughly the 35–60 crowd it's still strong; for much younger audiences, attention has moved elsewhere.

So: Google gives you intent and volume but broad targeting. Facebook gives you precise targeting but no built-in intent. Each has half of the ideal equation.

## **Why Could ChatGPT Ads Be the Biggest Opportunity Since the Facebook Heyday?**

Because AI platforms could combine *both* halves — intent, utility-level volume, *and* deep personal targeting — in one place.

Here's my thinking. People come to ChatGPT with a prompt, so there's **intent**, like Google. It's becoming a daily **utility** people can't imagine working without, so there's massive **volume**. And because it remembers your conversations and is moving toward persistent memory across threads, it can build a remarkably **detailed profile** of who you are — the kind of granular targeting that powered Facebook's best years. Put those together and, as I like to say, it's almost like if Meta and Google ads had a baby. My prediction is that early ChatGPT ads could be unusually efficient — reminiscent of the early Facebook-ads era before costs climbed. I'm not going to slap a specific number on it, but I'd expect this to become a place serious business owners invest.

There's a second tailwind: I think AI platforms will increasingly *reward creators and experts*. They need fresh data to crawl, and they have real incentives (including legal cover) to recommend credible, expert sources. So if you're putting out substantive content and you actually know your stuff, expect these systems to start surfacing you organically — which also makes any future paid placement more efficient.

## **A Word of Caution on Privacy**

This is more of a personal warning than a sales pitch: be careful what you share.

People treat ChatGPT like a therapist, typing things they'd never post publicly. That candor is exactly what makes the targeting so powerful — and exactly why you should be mindful. These platforms are saving this data. Use them, benefit from them, but go in with your eyes open about what you're handing over.

## **What Should Business Owners Do Right Now?**

Build content and dial in your messaging — because positioning now is what lets you win when these platforms mature.

Concretely: put out as much quality content as you can, leaning into long-form, and get crystal clear on who your person is and what your offer does for them. AI-driven distribution is increasingly deciding what to show based on content *relevance and quality* for a specific person — not audience size or hashtags, which are becoming archaic signals. The clearer and more consistent your messaging, the better these algorithms understand who you're for. Do that now, and when AI advertising fully arrives, the platforms will already understand who you are and who you're best suited to reach — which only drives your costs down.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### ***Is ChatGPT going to have ads?***

OpenAI has signaled it's moving toward allowing advertising on ChatGPT. Like most fast-scaling tech companies, it needs durable revenue, and advertising is a proven path once a platform reaches utility-level daily usage. Exactly how ads appear and how they're labeled will shape how users respond — especially given that people come to ChatGPT expecting an answer, not a list of options.

### ***Why could ChatGPT ads be a big opportunity for businesses?***

Because AI platforms may combine the strengths of the two biggest ad models: Google's search-style intent and utility-level volume, plus Facebook-style detailed targeting from the personal profiles these tools build over time. In my view, that combination could make early ChatGPT ads unusually efficient for businesses — though it's a prediction, and the specifics will depend on how the platforms roll it out.

### ***How are ChatGPT ads different from Google and Facebook ads?***

Google ads are intent-based with broad, pricier targeting; Facebook ads have precise targeting but no built-in purchase intent. ChatGPT ads could blend both — users arrive with intent (a prompt), use the platform as a daily utility (volume), and the platform builds a detailed profile of them (targeting). That's why I describe AI ads as combining the best of both worlds.

### ***Will running ads on ChatGPT hurt the trust in its answers?***

It's a legitimate concern. The promise of an AI platform is a curated answer, so when paid placement enters that answer, some users will question whether a recommendation is genuinely best or simply purchased. How transparently platforms separate paid from organic results will largely determine whether they preserve that trust.

### ***What should I do now to prepare for AI advertising?***

Produce substantial, quality content — especially long-form — and dial in your messaging so it speaks clearly to your specific ideal client. AI distribution increasingly favors relevance and quality over audience size or hashtags. Positioning yourself now means the platforms will already understand who you are and who you serve, which sets you up for both organic recommendations and more efficient ads later.
