**Your Left or Right Brained Nature is Not a Death Sentence**

Have you noticed how we are so quick to put ourselves and others into boxes?

"I can't learn how to be more creative because that's just not how my left-brain works."

Or..."Wow, I really need to find an operations-minded person for my business because my right-brained tendencies just don't cooperate."

For many of my youthful years, I flailed around, stuck and unable to put rubber to road.

My first "real" boss out of college told me if I could ever sink my teeth into something I'd be damned good at it. (verbatim)

My mentor between the ages of around 18-23 nicknamed me "Gemi", as in Gemini, due to my flakiness and inconsistencies.

For those that didn't know me in those years, those two statements will come as quite the surprise as many people now guess I'm a left-brainer.

So, when I met Allen and was introduced to his concepts about people being able to learn how to be more creative, I didn't just believe it was possible, I KNEW it!

You see, without delving into the crazy research I've done on neuroplasticity or literally rewiring neural pathways to quite literally rewrite the brain, let's just stay at a fundamental level and say we become skilled at what we commit our time, effort and energy too.

Now, do I believe some of us have a predisposition to be more uniquely skilled at various initiatives than others? Certainly, yes.

But like Allen said in our discussion, Mozart wasn't born composing world-renowned conciertos.

Through rigorous and very early training, he was already well on his way by the age of 17.

The question, in my humble opinion, lies in how we continue to broaden our skill sets while continuing to maximize our strengths.

As a startup founder and someone whom has coached and mentored several younglings, I can tell you this...

Very few of them wanted to adopt new skills required to meet the task at hand.

I see founders raising money without a mobile responsive website (If they have a website at all).

I hear beckoning cries for Tech Co-Founders when they haven't the slightest clue who they intend to serve, how the intend to make money and a viable road map to even remotely reach a point of revenue generation, let alone scaling with ferocity in the black.

## **What Should You Be Learning, Then?**

The million dollar question, right?

Do I expect you to become a TrackMaven of your own like Allen, assessing big data like a modern day Einstein in a lab?

NO WAY!

As an entrepreneur, your job is to build big companies, hire hundreds of people and figure out what the hell you're doing in the process.

Don't feel bad about that last one, I can still admit I have no idea what I'm doing even when I know what I'm doing.

What I would set a goal on is to have a basic, high level, macro understanding of Allen's concepts in the book.

For example, I read Tony Robbin's Money: Master the Game.

I'll never be a Ray Dalio or The Oracle from Omaha, Warren Buffett, but that;s okay!

I can still conceptualize their ideas in the book, apply them to my life and then eventually hire a fiduciary to come in and help manage my portfolio.

## **Here's the Bottom Line**

When you don't understand something at a fundamental level, how can you ever expect to know what's truly going on in your company?

I got lazy once in my first business.

You see, I had taken the approach of learn, do a little bit of it myself until I understood the basic premise and then outsource or delegate.

Often times, our training materials were second-to-none because I was like a former guest on Undercover Boss!

I knew what the job required because I, myself, had done it.

My expectations were reasonable and I knew when someone was giving me hogwash or, in some cases, trying to rip us off!

But just once I thought to myself, what if I just outsource our Google Adwords to a company we're already established with and has done great web development work for us in the past?

Surely this too is right up there avenue and they have come to understand what I expect in a third-party provider.

Three months later and we realized they not only had blown through a $20,000 budget but were sending the bulk of that paid traffic to a 404 page.

Meaning, they essentially shot highly expensive missiles into the vast, blue, ocean.

Had I taken the time to learn and understand Google Adwords, even at a macro level, I could have known what I was doing enough to have frequent conversations with them on the progress or instruct one of my employees to keep a watchful eye on it.

But I trusted blindly and got burned like a high school freshman on Spring Break with no sunscreen after passing out from their first rendezvous with ole Captain Jose Cuervo.

Lesson learned...

## **Making it Simple and Applicable**

So, maybe you don't roll out of bed with the inspiration to go paint a ceiling in some famous museum.

Maybe you skip right through those YouTube ads like 90% of the population or have completely cut the cord so you don't even see television commercials anymore.

Allen said something I hope you took note of and what I believe makes this simple for everyone.

Imitation is the name of the game!

Behind every great entrepreneur or marketer is an army of people dedicated to seeing initiatives through and studying their ideal archetypes or avatars like they would a new species discovered in the wild.

Leverage this, capitalize on their abundance of research and resources, notice the trends and be more intentional about not just what they're doing but WHY they're doing it.

I took a film class in college and I swear it was one of my top 5 favorite classes of all time.

We analyzed movies, frame by frame, and wow did I not realize just how much thought and preparation goes into each scene.

They are subtly telling their audience something at every single moment throughout the film.

Things you would never, ever notice.

(By the way, go watch The Godfather: Part 1 and notice something bad happens every single time they show oranges in the scene just prior. It's insane!)

But here's the problem, it is the things we would never ever notice that largely get us so emotionally hitched to what we're watching.

I'll give it to you this way...

Have you ever watched an indie film on a low budget and just completely fell into a snore-fest because it lacks that same pizzazz as a big budget film?

You can kind of tell it's a good, possibly even great, story but there's just something missing that you oh so enjoy about the latest Marvel film.

Well, obviously much of it has to do with equipment, acting prowess and massive CGI budgets but next time you watch one of those films notice the lighting, pay attention to the story arc, the character development, the camera angles, objects juxtaposed against one another or even the way the sunlight either shines or fails to shine on an actors face.

These are the subtleties that are overlooked but it's also where Directors make the big bucks.

Why?

Because it's showing off their skilled ability to tap into the psychology of their viewers.

It's why we cry about characters we know are completely fictitious or even leave thinking they actually exist in the real world.

By the way, I'm convinced the characters from both The Office and Parks and Recreation are out there living their lives as we saw on TV. (Kidding...Kind of)

Back to the matter at hand. When Allen says take note of these stories being told, the commercials being played and the marketing efforts on Facebook, you have to be a psychological detective and dig deep into the deeper meaning of WHY this is working and HOW it's reaching people on a meaningful level.

## **At the End of the Day, People are Tired of Buying CRAP!**

I'm not the first to say it and I definitely won't be the last.

There's too many people just selling stuff for the sake of selling it.

It may or may not be a good product or service, who knows?

But putting together a marketing effort with a pitch of buy my stuff because it's cheap is the sort of thing that gave used car salesmen a bad rap.

We have to be better than that.

We're smarter than that.

The world deserves more than that.

People are too smart in 2018 to fall for that nonsense and further, we are severely diminishing our chances at real, tangible success with less-than-stellar campaigns.

You may find a small group of people just throw up their arms and say, "what the heck", I'll give it a shot. But is that really a scalable model?

Wouldn't it be amazing to have people get to the end of your video ad and be anxiously awaiting the link to purchase like a kid chasing the ice cream truck down the street when the silly jingle blares through the neighborhood?

That, THAT, is how we create impact and transform our businesses and lives.

That is a goal worth chasing.

But it starts with you making a commitment to yourself to get out of your own way and quit telling yourself you fit into a box that others have identified for you.

Do you even know what being left or right brained even means outside of what you've heard others tell you about it?

My guess is probably not unless you're a brain nerd like me of which I have to be because I'm involved in a neuro startup.

Break your own shackles, look for the blueprint, and recognize it only takes even a basic level of understanding to see the needle really move forward in your life.
