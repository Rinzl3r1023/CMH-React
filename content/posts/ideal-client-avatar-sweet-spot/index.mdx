Here's a problem I see constantly. A coach or service provider creates three ideal client avatars. They list all the attributes of each one. Then they try to write marketing that speaks to all three at the same time.

And the result? Vague messaging that resonates with no one.

Sound familiar? Kim and I did this for years. It took us a while to realize the problem. You're trying to be specific, but the method you're using is pulling you toward being general. The fix is a concept I call the ICA Sweet Spot — and once you see it, you can't unsee it.

## **Why Does Building Avatar Lists Lead to Vague Messaging?**

Because a list of attributes for three different people is still three different people.

When you try to write content that captures Amber and Naen and Danielle all at once, you end up softening everything. You avoid the specific language that would click for Amber because it might not land for Danielle. And so you end up with broad statements that technically apply to all three — but resonate deeply with none of them.

You started with the right intention. You tried to get specific. But in trying to cover all three, you got broader again. It's one of the most common traps in marketing, and it happens to experienced business owners every day.

## **What Is the ICA Sweet Spot?**

The ICA Sweet Spot is the overlapping center of a Venn diagram across all your avatars.

Here's the exercise. Write out all the attributes of each avatar — let's say Amber, Naen, and Danielle. Then draw three overlapping circles. Push yourself to answer this question: what do all three of them share? Not the surface-level stuff like demographics. The deeper psychographics — values, mindset, approach to their business, what they actually care about.

That overlap is your sweet spot. It's the set of shared characteristics that makes someone a great fit for what you do. And it's what your messaging should center on.

### What Does the Sweet Spot Look Like in Practice?

For Chris and Kim, the sweet spot across their primary two avatars — Amber and Naen — came down to five things.

**Mission-driven.** They're not just in business to make money. They're building something with purpose — legacy, impact, a life that aligns with their values. That matters to them.

**Already has traction.** They're not complete beginners. They've been at this long enough to have some results, some clients, some sense of what they're building.

**All-in mentality.** They're not dabbling. They're committed. They don't treat their business like a side project.

**Self-motivated.** They don't need external accountability every week. They show up ready to work.

**Wants advanced strategies.** They've done the basics. They're ready for what comes next.

That's the sweet spot. When Chris and Kim speak to that person — that specific combination of characteristics — the right people hear it and immediately say "that's me."

### What Happens When an Avatar Doesn't Fit?

Sometimes you find that one of your avatars doesn't share enough with the others. That's valuable information.

For Chris and Kim, their third avatar — Danielle — sometimes needed external motivation and often felt unfocused. She's mission-driven and wants to grow, but she's not quite at the stage where their core coaching programs serve her best. So they eliminated her from the primary focus.

That doesn't mean she goes nowhere. She might be a great fit for a lower-tier offer — a subscription or a done-for-you content program, for example — where she can build momentum before she's ready for higher-level coaching. But wedging her into the main coaching programs wasn't serving her or the team.

Narrowing your focus to your actual sweet spot often makes your business feel lighter. And it makes your results for the right clients significantly better.

## **What Is an Anti-Avatar and Why Does It Matter?**

An anti-avatar is the person you are the worst possible fit for.

For Chris and Kim, that person resists feedback. They believe they already know everything. They're uncoachable, and the coaching dynamic breaks down quickly with them. When that person shows up in a program, it affects the whole group — the community, the energy, the quality of everyone's experience.

Defining the anti-avatar does two things. First, it helps you get even clearer on what you DO want. The antithesis of an uncoachable client is a self-motivated, curious, action-taking one — which sounds a lot like the sweet spot you're already building around.

Second, it enables you to "call people up" in your marketing. When you're clear about who you're not for, you can say so honestly. Something like: "If you think you already know everything and you're not open to feedback, this probably isn't the program for you." That sentence repels the wrong fit. But it also tells the right person: this is a space for people who are serious. And they lean in.

Think of it as throwing stones at a type of situation, not at a specific person. You're describing an experience your ideal client wants to avoid — not attacking anyone. Done right, it creates affinity. It says: we protect this space, and you're going to be surrounded by people like you.

## **How Do You Research Your Ideal Client?**

The best ICA clarity comes from real conversations with real people.

Here are several ways to gather it:

- **Interview your best clients.** Ask them why they chose you over someone else. Ask what they were struggling with before they found you. Ask what they care most about in their business and life. The language they use in those conversations becomes your marketing language.
- **Insight surveys.** Send a short survey to your email list with a small incentive — even a $5 gift card — for completing it. Ask what they're working on, what's holding them back, and what they most want to achieve.
- **Instagram polls and stories.** Quick, low-effort, and surprisingly revealing. A few well-placed questions can surface patterns across your audience fast.
- **Get in front of your people.** Early in their moving company, Chris and Kim literally went to the places their ideal clients hung out and asked questions. It sounds basic. But it works.

Anthropologie built their entire brand experience around one specific fictional person: a woman they named Julia Roberts. Everything in the store — the layout, the visual merchandising, the product selection, the marketing — passed one test: would Julia want this? That level of specificity built a cult-like following.

You can do the same thing. Give your sweet spot avatar a name. Then run every content decision through that person.

## **Why Does Profitability Matter More Than Popularity?**

Because 25 aligned people can build your business. Twenty-five thousand unaligned people can't.

This is one of the most important mindset shifts in all of marketing, and it's one Chris talks about constantly: profitability over popularity. Most people chase views, follower counts, and engagement metrics. But those numbers only matter if the right people are behind them.

If you post a video and only 25 people watch it — but all 25 of them are exactly the person you built your sweet spot around — those 25 people represent real potential clients, real referrals, real revenue. That's not a failure. That's precision.

Here's proof of what alignment produces. Naen — one of the actual people behind the avatar in this framework — made her first six figures in her first 12 months of business after working with Chris and Kim. Not because of luck. Because of alignment. She was self-motivated, mission-driven, and ready for advanced strategies. The system worked because she was the right fit, and the fit was clear from the beginning.

When you know your sweet spot, your marketing gets sharper, your clients get better results, and your referrals multiply. And then, when you add paid advertising, everything compounds. The message is already working. The ads just turn up the volume.

## **Key Takeaways**

- Building separate avatar lists and trying to speak to all of them at once creates vague, all-encompassing messaging — even when you started with the intention to be specific.
- The ICA Sweet Spot is the overlapping psychographics across all your avatars, found through a Venn diagram exercise.
- Center your messaging around the shared traits in the middle, not the full list for any one avatar.
- When an avatar doesn't fit your primary offer, refer them to a better fit — don't force the match.
- The anti-avatar exercise clarifies who you don't want to work with, which sharpens who you do. It also enables "calling people up" in content through honest, non-abrasive positioning.
- Profitability over popularity: 25 aligned people are more valuable than thousands of misaligned ones.
- Real ICA clarity comes from talking to your best clients — their language becomes your marketing.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### ***What is an ideal client avatar sweet spot?***

The ideal client avatar sweet spot is the set of overlapping psychographic characteristics shared across all of your best-fit client avatars. Instead of trying to market to each avatar separately — which leads to broad, diluted messaging — you center your marketing on the traits they all share. That intersection is your sweet spot, and it's what your content, offer, and positioning should speak to directly.

### ***Why does trying to speak to multiple avatars at once hurt your marketing?***

Because when you try to cover every attribute of every avatar in a single message, you soften everything. You end up with statements that technically apply to all of them but resonate strongly with none. You started with the intention to be specific, but the method pulled you back toward being general. The sweet spot exercise solves this by giving you a single, specific center to build around instead of a broad list to accommodate.

### ***What is an anti-avatar in marketing?***

An anti-avatar is the person you are the worst possible fit for — the client who would have a bad experience with you and whose presence in your programs would drain you and disrupt others. Defining your anti-avatar helps you get clearer on who you do want to serve. It also enables you to create content that honestly repels the wrong fit while attracting the right one, which is a key strategy for building a strong, aligned community.

### ***How do you research your ideal client avatar?***

The most reliable methods are direct conversations and real feedback. Interview your best clients and ask why they chose you, what they were struggling with before, and what they most care about. Run insight surveys to your email list with a small incentive. Use Instagram polls and stories for quick pattern data. And get out in front of your people — Chris and Kim conducted in-person interviews in their early days, asking their target audience questions directly.

### ***Is a saturated market a reason not to enter a niche?***

No. Competition in a market actually validates demand — it proves people want the thing you're offering. The challenge in a saturated market isn't whether to compete, but how to differentiate. That's where the ICA Sweet Spot comes in. When you know exactly who your specific person is and what they care about, you can build messaging and offers that resonate deeply with them, even in a crowded space.
