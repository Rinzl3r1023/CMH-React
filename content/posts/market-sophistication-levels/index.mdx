Let me ask you something. Are you putting out content consistently but not seeing sales? Are your posts getting some engagement but not converting? There's a good chance the problem isn't your effort. The problem is your message level.

Most content creators and coaches are speaking at the wrong level of awareness for their market. They're saying the right things — but to the wrong version of their audience. Once you fix that, everything changes. Engagement goes up. Conversions go up. And the right people start finding you.

This framework comes from Eugene Schwartz, one of the great direct-response marketers of the last century. His book *Breakthrough Advertising* laid out five levels of market sophistication. I've used this concept — through my own Content to Customers lens — to help clients completely transform their results. Let me walk you through each level. Then I'll tell you which one most coaches are in, and what to do about it.

## **What Are the Five Levels of Market Sophistication?**

The five levels represent how aware your audience is — of their problem, of solutions that exist, and of you specifically. Each level needs a different kind of message.

Here are the five, in order:

1. **Unaware** — your audience doesn't know they have a problem yet, or doesn't know a solution like yours exists.
2. **Problem Aware** — they know the problem but don't know what solutions are available.
3. **Solution Aware** — they know solutions exist, but they haven't found the right one yet. This is where most coaches sit.
4. **Brand Aware** — they already know who you are. You just need to stay top of mind.
5. **Deal Aware** — they know you, they're ready to buy, and they just want the right offer.

Where you fall on this scale completely changes how you should write your content, ads, and offers.

## **What Is an Unaware Market?**

An unaware market has no idea your solution exists — or doesn't yet feel the problem you solve.

Think about the first time most people heard about cryptocurrency or the metaverse. There was no existing demand to tap. You had to explain what it was before anyone could care. So in an unaware market, most of your messaging focuses on education. You're not leading with your offer. You're explaining why the problem matters and why your solution is something they should pay attention to.

Most coaches and service providers are NOT in an unaware market. But it's worth knowing what it looks like. If you're early on a truly new concept, expect to spend most of your time just getting people up to speed.

## **What Is a Problem Aware Market?**

A problem aware audience knows something is wrong. But they don't know what solutions exist.

A personal example: my wife and I discovered mold in our home recently. It's a brand-new house, only two years old. I was fully aware of the problem — I could see it, I could feel the effects. But I had no idea what to do. I didn't even know the term "mold remediation." So I wasn't searching for a specific service. I was just trying to understand what was happening to me.

In a problem-aware market, your messaging should start with the problem. Meet people where they are. Name what they're experiencing. Then introduce your solution. The mistake most coaches make here is jumping straight to their offer. But the audience isn't ready for that yet. They're still processing the problem. So earn the right to present your solution by acknowledging theirs first.

## **What Is a Solution Aware Market?**

A solution aware audience already knows solutions exist. They've probably tried one or two. They may have been disappointed. And right now, they have no clear reason to choose you over anyone else.

This is where roughly 90% of coaches and service providers live. And it's where most marketing goes wrong.

### Why Undifferentiated Messaging Becomes Noise in a Solution Aware Market

Think about it from the audience's side. They've heard about massage therapy, online therapy, coaching programs, and marketing courses a hundred times. So when you show up saying "I help coaches grow their business," they have no reason to stop scrolling. They've heard that before. They may have even paid for it before and felt let down.

In a solution aware market, your message has to answer three questions clearly and fast: Who do you serve specifically? How is your approach different from every other option out there? Why does that difference matter to this exact person?

If you can't answer those three questions in your content, your ads, and on your website — you're putting out noise. And noise doesn't build trust. It doesn't create super fans. And it definitely doesn't convert.

### What Differentiation Looks Like in Practice

FUBU is a good example. Daymond John built the brand around one idea: "for us, by us." It wasn't just clothing. It was clothing that said something about who you were and where you came from. That specificity built massive loyalty. It wasn't trying to be Nike or Gap. It was for a specific culture, and that culture rallied around it.

Your differentiation doesn't have to be radical. It just has to be real and relevant to your person. Maybe you're the only coach in your niche who focuses on a specific demographic. Maybe your methodology uses a framework no one else teaches. Maybe your client experience is dramatically more personal than the big name in your space. Whatever it is, be overt about it. Don't make your audience guess.

## **What Does Getting This Right Actually Do for Your Business?**

The results can be dramatic — and they happen faster than most people expect.

I have an anonymous client in the health and wellness services space. Before we worked together, he was doing around $800,000 a year. His messaging was solid, but it wasn't differentiated. He was in a solution-aware market and speaking in a way that blended into everyone else in his category.

So we repositioned his offer. We made his messaging specific to who he serves and how his approach is different. We didn't run elaborate ad campaigns. We didn't build complex funnels. We just fixed the message.

Here's what happened. His revenue jumped to between $1.1 and $1.2 million. His average customer charge went from around $800 to around $3,000 — more than triple. And his clients started referring their friends with a genuine enthusiasm that didn't exist before. The content converted better. The website converted better. The current clients became louder advocates. All of it moved because the message got clear.

Now we're ready to add ads. And that's when things get really interesting. Because ads are just gasoline on the fire. But first, you have to build the fire.

## **What Are Brand Aware and Deal Aware Markets?**

These last two levels are less relevant for most coaches and service providers right now. But they're worth knowing.

**Brand Aware** is where Arby's lives. You already know who Arby's is. They don't have to explain what a sandwich is or convince you to eat fast food. They just have to remind you they exist. Online therapy platforms like BetterHelp operate here too. They can say their name and people know what they're getting. That kind of messaging is cheap to run at scale — but only because they've already done the work of becoming known. Most of us aren't there yet.

**Deal Aware** is for established brands running promotions. Think Domino's advertising a deal. They skip every other level because everyone already knows who they are, what they sell, and why they'd buy it. All they need to do is give you a reason to order tonight.

If you're not yet at the brand-aware level, that's perfectly fine. In fact, it means you have an advantage: you can be specific and targeted in ways a big brand simply cannot.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### ***What are market sophistication levels?***

Market sophistication levels are a framework from direct-response marketer Eugene Schwartz that describe how aware your audience is of their problem, available solutions, and you specifically. There are five levels: Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Brand Aware, and Deal Aware. Knowing which level your market is at tells you what your messaging needs to focus on to actually convert.

### ***Which market sophistication level are most coaches and service providers in?***

Most are in the Solution Aware level. Their audience already knows that solutions like theirs exist — coaching, therapy, marketing help, health services — but has no clear reason to choose one provider over another. In that environment, undifferentiated messaging gets ignored. The key is to be specific about who you serve and how your approach is different from every other option in the market.

### ***What is the difference between problem aware and solution aware marketing?***

In a problem aware market, your audience knows something is wrong but doesn't know what solutions exist. Your messaging should meet them at the problem first, then introduce your solution. In a solution aware market, they already know solutions exist — they may have tried some — so your messaging needs to clearly differentiate your specific approach from all the others they've encountered.

### ***How do you differentiate your offer in a solution aware market?***

Answer three questions clearly and fast in your content, ads, and website: Who do you serve specifically? How is your approach different from other options? Why does that difference matter to your person? The more specific your answers, the stronger your signal. Vague messaging blends into the category. Specific messaging builds faster trust and attracts the right people.

### ***Do ads work better once you've differentiated your offer?***

Yes — significantly. Ads work by amplifying what already converts. If your message isn't resonating first, more traffic just means more wasted budget. But once your differentiated message is working organically — in content, on your website, through referrals — ads become extremely powerful. Think of the message as the fire and the ads as the gasoline. Build the fire first.
