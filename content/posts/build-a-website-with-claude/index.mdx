## **Why "Build Me a Website" Gives You Generic AI**

Type "build me a website" into a fresh AI and you'll get a functional skeleton — decent structure, placeholder-ish copy, reasonable patterns. And it'll be generic, because the model is working from broad best practices with zero context about you as a specific operator in a specific market.

That's how you end up in what I call iteration hell. The AI builds something, you read it and think *that doesn't sound like me*, you send it back, it adjusts, it still misses on positioning, you go again. Five, six, seven rounds — because every cycle, the AI is guessing at the variables that actually matter: your ideal client, your voice, how you're different from competitors, your sales psychology.

The output is technically correct and strategically hollow. It's a website. It's just not *your* website.

## **How Do Frameworks Make AI Build a Better Website?**

Frameworks remove the guessing. When your ideal client profile, your messaging, your competitive positioning, and your sales method are loaded in *before* the build, the AI isn't interpreting what you want — it's executing from a blueprint.

That's the difference between a generic site and one that converts: every decision — copy hierarchy, visual flow, calls to action, positioning statements — is shaped by work that's already proven itself with your clients. The messaging map informs the copy. The ICA psychographics inform the tone. The sales framework informs the structure. So it lands close to right on the first attempt instead of after seven rounds of "almost."

It's the same idea as connecting Claude to your project tools: you're giving the model the thinking that normally only lives in your head.

## **Is Building From Scratch Harder Than a Revamp?**

Yes — and it's the better test. A revamp gives the AI existing brand DNA, messaging, and positioning to work from. A brand-new product has none of that.

I put this to the test on the SPARC site itself — a product that was barely a month old, still half-formed in my head. There was no existing copy to reverse-engineer from. It still landed on the first pass — which tells me the frameworks aren't just good for *optimizing* an existing brand. They're generative. They can build new positioning from nothing but your strategic patterns and ICA work.

## **The Real Risk: AI Slop and Sounding Like Everyone Else**

There's a threat most people building with AI right now aren't thinking about. As everyone reaches for the same base models with the same generic prompts, the internet is filling with what I call AI slop — sites and copy that are structurally identical. Same cadence, same positioning moves, same value props, different names pasted in.

And clients feel it. When someone reads 50 coaching sites that all sound the same, your positioning collapses into noise. You might feel ahead today because you're using AI while others aren't — but the moment everyone's using it, the edge is gone, and you're indistinguishable.

Frameworks are the way out. They take your unique positioning and *amplify* it instead of flattening it. In a market about to be flooded with undifferentiated AI output, sounding unmistakably like you isn't a nice-to-have — it's how you stay visible.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Can you build a website with Claude?**

Yes. Claude can generate full page mockups — copy, structure, and layout — that you or a developer can then make live on a platform like WordPress. The quality jumps dramatically when you feed Claude your frameworks (ideal client, messaging, positioning, sales method) so it builds a site specific to you rather than a generic template.

### **Why does AI build generic websites?**

Because a plain "build me a website" prompt gives the AI no context about your market, voice, or positioning, so it falls back on broad best practices. The result is structurally fine but generic, and it usually takes several rounds of revisions to get close to your vision. Supplying your frameworks up front removes that guessing.

### ***How long does it take to build a website with AI?***

With frameworks supplying the context, mockups can be produced in well under an hour — in my case, around 14 pages in about 45 minutes and four sites in roughly two hours. Turning a mockup into a live page still takes a developer some time (about 20–30 minutes per page in our process), but the overall timeline collapses from months to hours.

### **Do I still need a developer or designer?**

For now, usually yes — to take the AI-generated mockup and make it live, handle technical setup, and polish. The shift is that the heavy creative and strategic lifting (copy, positioning, structure) is done first-pass, so the human role moves to execution and refinement rather than building from a blank page.

### **Will AI-built websites all start to look the same?**

That's the real risk — "AI slop," where everyone using the same models with generic prompts produces near-identical sites. The defense is differentiation: building on your own frameworks, voice, and positioning so your site reflects you specifically rather than a generic template the AI produces by default.
