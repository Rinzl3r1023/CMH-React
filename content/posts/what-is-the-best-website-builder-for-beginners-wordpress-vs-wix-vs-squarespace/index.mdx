## What is the best website builder for beginners?

## **Wordpress vs Wix vs SquareSpace**

**Chris Michael Harris**, Entrepreneur

Alright, so today's question comes from Tony and Greg, and they want to know,
****

> **What is the best website builder for beginners?**

They've heard about WordPress, but they're wondering about Wix and Squarespace as well.
So basically, we're doing a WordPress versus Wix versus Squarespace in terms of what website builder you should use to start.
So let's begin.

It's going to come down to, first of all, your level of sophistication with technology. So if you are pretty good with technology, you pick things up quickly, and you're willing to do the work to learn what WordPress can provide, WordPress is still the king for running a business.

And here are a couple of reasons as to why.

## **WORDPRESS WAS ORIGINALLY MADE FOR BLOGGERS**

According to their constructions, WordPress will rank well above a site built on Wix or Squarespace.

That means that your content, as you put it out, is cleaner than the way that search engines can read the content that's on it. And thus, it's going to give you priority over other similar sites.

I don't always tell people not to consider Wix or Squarespace. The reason being is that I would rather see you make progress and stay on the sidelines with WordPress.

## **THE PLUGINS AND CONNECTIVITY THAT WORDPRESS PROVIDES**

The plugins and the seamless connectivity that WordPress provides are second to none.

WordPress is a library of plugins that you can add for the different functions you want your site to have. Comparing it to the others is not even close.

The connectivity that WordPress provides as your business grows, you're going to want to have that. You're going to be able to integrate with things like scheduling or kick things to an email marketing tool or embed a manual pixel from Facebook to run ads.

So in terms of growth, you'll probably eventually have to migrate to WordPress anyway to have more of the advanced things that your business might require that Wix or Squarespace may or may not provide.

It may, and you may never have to migrate over, and that's fine. I'm just saying that it does provide limitations to what WordPress provides.

## **PROS OF WIX AND SQUARESPACE**

As mentioned, Wix and Squarespace, in terms of an out-of-the-box builder, you're good to go. You can sign up, grab a template, add your pictures, and have a site done within an hour. Literally, it's that simple.

And you could take your domain that you purchased from someplace and embed it right there, and they do all the hosting and everything for you.

## **FACTOR TO CONSIDER**

If you build on Wix and Squarespace and you migrate your site over to WordPress, you want to make sure you have a developer do that for you.

Because if you don't do it right, re-indexing your site to Google can drop your rank. So if you built to number one overall in your geographic area with your local business, and then you migrate your site over to WordPress because of the fantastic things that WordPress can do, you may drop a few spots temporarily while you build up the credibility again of your website on WordPress.

## **OVER TO YOU**

Regardless of what builder you use, you're probably going to need a developer to help at some point. I recommend Upwork or Fiverr as resources to help specifically with WordPress. Or if something happens with your site and it crashes, or somebody hacks it or whatever, you've already got that resource established that you can go back to and ask for help. So good practice to have, regardless of whichever one you decide.
