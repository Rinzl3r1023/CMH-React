## **Why Can't We Get Ahead In Life, Financially!?**

When we fail to plan we plan to fail, right?

That saying has never been more true in this episode. Truth is, most of us probably don't even truly know how much we're spending on a monthly basis, we just know if there's enough money to cover rent, bills and maybe some play money if we're lucky.

In fact, it's been found that anywhere between 68-78% of Millennials will not be prepared for retirement if current trends prevail.

What's more is that over 80% of Americans currently do not have $2,000 in their checking or savings account to cover them in the event of a catastrophic occurrence. When you understand the behavioral patterns and compare that to the amount of recurring money in our accounts, it's easy to see where and how people slip so far into debt.

Once in debt and with no plan in place, it can be quite the feat indeed to dig yourself out of the metaphorical hole and get feet back on solid and financially stable ground,

So, what do we do?

Well, it certainly doesn't involve what most of us resort to by burying our heads in the sand and pretending it's going to somehow go away.

Tony Robbins, whom I reference frequently throughout this episode, says finance is one area in particular where oblivion truly isn't bliss...It's pain and suffering.

Now, I get it and I've been there. As an upstart entrepreneur in my early days of trying to make things work and figure out how to do this thing called life successfully, I listened to some horrendous advice from my personal banker to put all of my business expenses on my personal credit cards.

When my business didn't take off immediately as I expected, I saw my credit score drop over 250 points and with my interest rates it truly became a domino affect. I didn't plan well enough, I just told myself I was doing what I had to do.

All the while, I was grocery shopping at Trader Joe's and had an office space that was costing me more than my business was currently generating. This didn't even take into account any of my personal expenditures.

Soon thereafter, it wasn't a rare occasion for my power to get shut off, creditors to blow up my phone day and night and overall the general feeling of overwhelm and chaos.

## **Your Personal Life Should Be Treated Like a Business**

It wasn't until I started treating my personal life like a business that I got things back in order.

No more nights out drinking with friends. The markup on alcohol is outrageous. Eating out had to go, fast food wasn't good for me anyways.

With budgeting tools like Mint, I felt I could once again empower myself to not just know where my money was going but allocate my funds accordingly.

When my business finally boomed, and boy did it ever boom with growth of over 1,000% to $500,000, I was able to pay off my credit card debts and get my head back above water.

Now, when it comes to saving I have a bit of a different perspective than the conventional strategy.

The interest rates with banks is far less than 1% so you're really not going to see the needle move a whole lot by just sinking your money into a savings account.

But, at the same time, investing is a full-time gig and I've seen way too many people get burned in the stock market; losing as much as half of their nest egg in a matter of days during the 2008 housing crash.

But what if there was a way for individuals to participate in the upside and benefit of investing while simultaneously protecting their downside and minimizing their losses. As in, historically speaking, gains of 7-10% while limiting losses to under 3% in any given year?

I was intrigued but didn't believe it to be true, to be totally candid.

Investing is what you did when you could afford to take a hit, not what you did as someone putting everything they had into growing a startup business.

Boy, did Money: Master the Game by Tony Robbins change my mind forever!

In the book, Tony interviews some of the greatest investment minds of not just our time but ANY time. Guys like The Oracle from Omaha, Warren Buffett, and Ray Dalio, whom has been dubbed one of the most successful hedge fund managers of all time.

Both of them present a new way, a new game and a new strategy that even the common folk can play.

Call it an all-seasons plan or other similar variances but essentially it's a diversified portfolio across multiple asset classes that counteract the losses of other asset classes in any particular year.

It also entails buying low-cost index funds, owning a piece of the entire market, versus trying to speculate on individual stocks.

In other words, not getting fried on bank stocks when their values dropped to just 1 measly dollar in and around the '08 crash.

Tony's team went back and ran the numbers with these plans outlined in the book and their findings were astounding!

On average, your gains would have been between 7-10% annually and on average over a 75-90 year time frame.

Losses, when experienced, never exceeded 5%!

I was astounded...to say the least.

But Chris, how am I going to invest in a plan like this if I'm barely scraping by as it is!?

See topic number one of this blog entry.

We need to first get you eliminating waste. Essentially, things you can live without.

Maybe the tequila budget needs to be slightly adjusted or maybe you split a bottle with some friends and casually enjoy a night at the pool instead of an all-night rager downtown where it costs you $35 just to step foot in the door.

I read that millennials were currently spending right around $1,000/month on weekend partying.

That's $12,000/year!

Imagine if you stuck that money into a self-managed 401K and applied the Ray Dalio all-seasons plan.

What ends up happening is your money doubles roughly every 7 years, and that's assuming your not putting any more money in annually.

Pretty wild, right?

## **The 6 Basic Human Needs to Live an Amazing Life - Tony Robbins Model**

1. Certainty, Comfort - Despite our entrepreneurial desires and taste for rolling the dice, we all need to feel a sense of certainty and when we don't we experience overwhelm. Overwhelm, for an entrepreneur, is absolutely toxic. It not only clouds your mind and prevents you from taking the right steps to move forward but it also keeps you small, stripping you of the innovative and creative thinking that probably helped you achieve success to begin with.
2. Uncertainty or Variety - Sometimes, as business owners, we get so entrenched into what we're doing that it becomes our life. Maybe it's been decades since your last vacation or even a weekend outing with your significant other. Maybe a new challenge is necessary, some form of adventure or excitement to reignite your soul. Have you ever stepped out to do something you used to enjoy only to realize you left with way more zest and energy for life as a result? Even the slightest shift in your routine can sometimes be enough to spark a new fire.
3. Significance - Ever been in a situation where either you, a co-worker or maybe even a business partner didn't feel valued? The contributions weren't recognized and the work they did seemed to fall on a blind eye and def ears. More often than not, people will LEAVE those situations, even if highly compensated, because of their inherent need to feel valued, significant and important.
4. Love and Connection - Why do so many people still get married when the divorce rate is well over 50%? More often than not, people won't do something if there's even a 10% chance of failure, let alone an overwhelming probability. We need love and connection to fuel our souls. It's why we have domesticated animals and kept them inside of our homes, paying thousands of dollars in upkeep, pet fees, vet fees and pet sitters when we decide to take a trip.
5. Growth - How great do you feel about yourself when you're stuck on something? Probably doesn't make you feel like you want to climb mountains and blast through concrete walls. There's so much I teach entrepreneurs how to setup realistic goals in their businesses and lives to recognize growth and achievement for that very reason. When we fail to grow or recognize progress, we lose motivation and desire to continue.
6. Contribution - The most understated and undervalued of the six, by a landslide. You see, we lie and tell ourselves we have nothing to give until we've "made it," when it reality part of "making it" involves you giving along the way in whatever way you can. Some of the happiest and most proud moments we can experience and feel in our lives is when we give something to someone else. It lights our souls on fire, makes us feel a special connection with humanity and reminds us that just one person can truly make a difference if we put our minds to it.
