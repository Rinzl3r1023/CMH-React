## **A Rising Tide Lifts All Boats**

Does it matter where you live in order to build a successful startup?

Or rather, is there a best place to build a startup?

Man, that's such a tricky subject and one I'm truthfully going to do my best to answer.

But first, let's rewind the clocks back a few decades.

Have you ever heard someone say you went to New York City to see if you could make it?

That if you could make it there you could make it anywhere!

It's notions like this that previously led s to believe success was, to at least a certain degree, location specific.

Now, that doesn't mean you wouldn't find thriving industry outside of metropolitan areas, I'm merely using NYC as an example.

You went where the opportunity was.

Fast forward to 2018 and you'll find online business owners living the digital nomad lifestyle, traveling to wherever their heart's desire.

And, I might add, making significant money in the process.

The digital frontier has changed the game!

Tools like Skype allow us to have face-to-face connection.

In fact, I only recently met my business coach of nearly three years and I've never met my Doctor in person.

Literally, every single one of those meetings took place via Skype or some form of video conferencing.

It was quite bizarre to finally meet Bill, my coach, for the first time.

On one hand, you feel like you don't know the person but on the other you know you've shared some of your most meaningful moments as a business owner with this person.

It was like I knew him and yet I didn't know him at all.

Either way, once I got over the physical presence of actually seeing a person to put with my experiences it was business as usual.

My point is it's more than viable to run a successful business in your underwear and from your home...just don't let the camera show you're not wearing pants :D

## **But What's The Opportunity Cost?**

Okay, now this is where we go from is it feasible to is it the most impactful.

And this is where it get's trickier...immeasurably trickier.

And I also think it gets way more individualized.

So, let's tackle that one first.

If you would have asked me five years ago if I saw myself as the type of person that could work from anywhere I would have blurted out "NO" before you finished asking the question...

Truth be told, I had tried on several occasions and to no avail.

I just couldn't overcome the mental hurdle of my environment not being a place that was conducive to hard work and getting things done.

That was the place I relaxed, joked around and enjoyed my family...not the place I set the world on fire.

If I went on a trip, I'd have to find a coffee shop just to knock out some basic administrative functions but nothing substantial or needle moving.

Further, in both scenarios, I had to wear business clothes just to get myself in the right frame of mind!

You can imagine how ridiculous I looked sitting at my kitchen table in a three-piece suit..ha!

[via GIPHY](https://giphy.com/gifs/jtvedit-jtv-rafael-solano-Hqxir3mP8YNRC)

But, very slowly, I began to love my geographic freedom the digital world provided and I've now trained myself into thriving wherever I am.

I can be just as effective on my sofa as I can at a co-working space with other like-minded people.

And I did it by sticking to my routines and habits while also working on the mental discipline required to be a juggernaut, regardless.

That said, I do think it's possible for a good portion of you to accomplish the same and I'll be happy to cover what those habits look like in another post.

However, for some people, it's just not a good fit.

And that's okay!

It's about finding what works for you and putting yourself in situations to succeed.

Now, on to the matter of opportunity cost.

I do think it exists and we're now getting into the meat and potatoes of this discussion so buckle up...

People in general thrive when they have access to the support and resources they need to grow and think beyond their circumstances.

We built out first company to a multi-million dollar firm within a very short time frame and in a college town.

Truth be told, we were becoming a big fish in a very small pond.

Very few people, if any, knew what we were experiencing as a startup.

The third party providers we hired all seemed to fall short of what we needed.

One summer alone I threw $15,000 at an outsourced CFO to help us build an accounting infrastructure to accommodate accelerated growth.

By the end of that summer I had to cut ties because this person just flat out hadn't dealt with someone in our situation.

We were a completely different animal than your usual startup in a small, college town.

And I could go on for days and days about a variety of similar experiences in that town.

Finally, we decided to move our efforts to Atlanta.

A bigger city!

We'll finally get the resources we need, right!?

Wrong...

Same problem, different end of the spectrum.

In this case, they were very acclimated with larger corporations but the startup scene was still not where it needed to be as an ecosystem.

And so they were trying to cram enterprise solutions down a startups throat OR needed us to do more of the heavy lifting before we got to a point they felt comfortable in helping.

Now, I can already hear your objections through the text on this page.

Chris!

It's a digital world, why didn't you just use that Google machine to find someone elsewhere to help you?

And while I agree in most scenarios that that would be an effective route, it's not always that simple.

In other words, there are times, specifically with an emerging startup, where it can be a complex situation.

As in, your organization is growing beyond just you and the problems are bigger than just a one-time phone call.

Many times a service provider just couldn't grasp the magnitude of our needs without being in person.

When they came and visited and had a chance to sit down and see it for themselves...different ballgame.

They could SEE the business and conceptualize what we aimed to accomplish in a way that previously just wasn't possible.

Now, if you're a solopreneur, you may never see any of these challenges.

You may never hire a single person outside of you Virtual Assistant and you may just aim to do a couple hundred thousand dollars per year.

That's awesome!

And if that fits who you are and leaves you fulfilled, you're almost out of the woods...but not yet.

## **Community Gives You a Mental Edge**

The minute we moved to Atlanta my perspective changed.

In fact, my entire way of thinking changed.

In my small, college town, I felt like I was becoming the king of the world.

People knew me as the successful startup guy and invited me to come speak to their college classes.

I would get stopped on the street and asked questions.

Not as a celebrity, let's not blow things out of proportion, but definitely known in the community.

It was awesome!

I felt really amazing about myself.

And then we moved to Atlanta and I went back to the bottom of the totem pole.

My little tiny company that was grossing low seven figures paled in comparison to the corporate giants...many of whose buildings literally touched the sky.

And it didn't just stop there...

I would get invited to networking events and feel like I knew NOTHING.

These people were having such high level conversations compared to my college town.

I found myself asking way more questions than fielding them as I once had before.

We actually had an ongoing joke in Athens (the college town) that everyone I spoke with ended up turning into a coaching session.

And again, while I thought that was awesome, what I didn't realize until I left was just how truly small it was keeping me.

The way I thought, the way I dreamed and thus, the way I behaved.

Within weeks, I was recognizing areas of the company where we just flat out were missing the big picture.

We were acting like a little company because A. We were and B. Nothing, nor anyone, has pushed us otherwise.

And, important point to note, I am ALWAYS pushing myself so it wasn't like I just got comfortable.

It's bizarre and I'm probably not doing the best job of explaining just how radical the shift was.

If you wear glasses maybe you'll relate to this metaphor.

You remember when you received your first pair of glasses and headed home?

If you're like me, you didn't even realize trees had leaves!

You knew they were there but you just never could see them as vividly as you now could.

And no matter how hard you looked before, the standard was defined by the circumstances.

You had no idea anything else existed outside of what was your perceived reality of the world around you.

Until you put on your glasses and the world literally changed.

THAT...is probably the best way I can describe the awakening that took place.

What's more is that all my entrepreneurial friends that stayed back in Athens are largely still in the same place in their businesses...stuck.

And not just in business but in life.

## **And That's Because Growing is Intentional**

My wife and I made a decision...

If we experienced such a radical transformation from moving to Atlanta, what would it be like if we actually immersed ourselves in a thriving entrepreneurial community?

We kicked around the idea of San Francisco but opted for Austin.

While smaller and definitely not the powerhouse of San Francisco, we also wanted to be fiscally responsible and also take note of the trends.

Austin is experiencing a massive uptick.

In fact, Austin is one of, if not the most, fastest growing cities in the entire country.

Something like 50 new people move to this city every single day.

And the result...

Well, as I sit here and write this post from an amazing co-working space in the heart of downtown and surrounded by some of the most amazing minds I've ever met, is we feel like we belong.

You see, we didn't have to have this long and drawn out conversations about what we ACTUALLY did for work or HOW we made money.

People not only got it, they contributed to the conversation and probably connected us to someone else that could collaborate with us in the journey.

Within a matter of months, I was fully plugged in and contributing to the amazing ecosystem here.

I remember my first mastermind type group I sat in on and left with the exact same impression I had when I arrived in Atlanta.

It felt like, yet again, I had no idea what I was doing or talking about!

Some of these people, whom I now collaborate with, have been senior executives in Silicon Valley for some of the biggest companies on the planet.

Taking their efforts to the startup space, we now get to "play ball" together.

And so I'm left here thinking about the entrepreneur I was versus the one I am today.

To say there's NO comparison is literally the understatement of the century.

And that's amazing...as well as how it should be.

So, back to the original question we came here to answer...

Does community truly matter or can we just do our thing anywhere in the world?

So, here's my final feedback because I think that question is best for you to answer for yourself.

Have you been honest with yourself lately about what you want to accomplish and where you want to be in life?

Have you asked if you're giving yourself the best possible opportunities and resourced to succeed?

If that answer is yes, go YOU!

Seriously...

Many of my digital mentors live wherever they want!

They've put in their time, grew to a certain point and have now ventured off into the sunset but still continue to do their thing online.

They've also learned how to continue growing and pushing themselves, regardless of geographic location.

For me, I prefer a combination of the two...

I may work from home and travel the world with my amazing and beautiful wife but home base is a place where I know I can tap in and challenge myself to reach the next level.

And maybe that's not always here...maybe there is no "home."

Maybe home for you is just wherever the next pursuit lies.
