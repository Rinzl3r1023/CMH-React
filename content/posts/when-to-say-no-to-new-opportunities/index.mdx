## **To Do or Not To Do...That is the Question**

Don't you just love when a new opportunity presents itself and you can bask in the moment?

Like, really just feel the win that somebody now wants you involved with some thing that they could have chosen someone else for...

It's amazing!

We hear them talk about how the awesome reasons they want us involved...

How their vision can be furthered and how you are going to help them do that.

Or maybe it's a huge financial incentive that's going to leverage you and your family out of debt.

And then we leave.

And then the reality of actually HAVING to make a decision starts to set in.

Now, if you're in a bad predicament and you sought out this new opportunity, this is probably an easy answer.

But what I'm talking about is unexpected opportunities that arise when you're already in a good spot.

Happy with what you're doing, making good money and content with the progress being made.

And so we drive home or lay awake that night trying to envision was this new life would be.

If you're like me it probably revolves around how you intend to throw just one more ball into that juggling act.

You see, I love being a serial entrepreneur.

I'm multi-passionate.

I don't just love ONE thing.

In fact, what I love is growth, progress and seeing people succeed.

That can be applied in many ways, right?

Well, recently I've been bombarded with new opportunities.

Opportunities to join emerging startups, be involved with venture funds...the list goes on.

In fact, I'm up to around seven (or so) opportunities just in the past five months!

You know how many I said yes to?

Zero...

## **And Here's the Reason(s) Why**

I love these people and I love what they aim to accomplish.

And here's the thing I failed to mention...

Every single one of these opportunities has a lot of potential zeroes tied to the back end of those figures.

So, it's not like I'm just saying no because the money doesn't warrant my time.

It's literally quite the opposite.

Why say no, then?

Are you a crazy person, Chris?

Yes, but that doesn't apply to this particular instance ;)

The reason, and the reason you should consider always saying no, is I just can't see how it aligns with my long-term goals.

In some cases, it just didn't align with my values.

If you listen to my podcast and hear the things I have to say and then hear what some of these companies stood for or listened in on a private conversation, you would think there's no way this is the Chris Michael Harris I'm familiar with.

And that's important to me...like, REALLY important.

Not just for saving face with you but for aligning with my own values.

Who am I if I betray what I stand for?

What kind of leader and man can I expect to become if I sell out for the sake of money and an exciting opportunity?

Now, these weren't ALL value conflicts...in fact, some of them really do line up with my values.

But then maybe it doesn't align with my future goals.

Maybe it's a short-term wonder but long-term it's really going to derail some of my efforts.

In other words, my main thing would have to shift to some "other" thing.

## **And So We Find Why People Get Distracted**

We all know the type.

The person that's always clamoring this next thing is THE one!

This is the opportunity they've been waiting for.

Life will forever change and everyone was always wrong about them.

But you know the reality about opportunities?

We often create our own.

Through hard work, commitment and mastery, our skills rise us to the upper echelon of our field.

Think about that for a second.

When was the last time you listened to some expert or worked for a boss that hadn't mastered some unique skill?

If you have, you're probably not following the right people.

Now, that doesn't mean they have to just be good at that ONE thing.

The highest performers in the world have indeed mastered many areas of life.

But they did so by first learning how to master just one.

That gave them the template to repeat the process.

And so what you find is a pattern that doesn't resemble someone just leaping at new opportunities.

## **Here's What I Did and What You Can Easily Do Too**

My monkey mind can take over if I let it.

I can start imagining scenarios in my head and start convincing myself these visions of the future are a sign.

My vivid dreams come from the divine trying to convince me this is the right move.

But in reality, this isn't a crap shoot and if you are going to embrace this opportunity, you must have a process for doing so.

That is, if you want to get really good at decision making.

First, as mentioned, I see if this aligns with my values and future goals.

Check...

Next, write down what your life will look like with this new opportunity.

This includes what will have to be sacrificed, not just the good stuff.

We have a tendency of romanticizing the "new" stuff.

Then I want you to remove money from the equation.

What!?

But dude, that's why I'm considering this to begin with...

My guess is that's why you're about to make a bad decision.

And I'm not hear to tell you money is bad.

In fact, I'm trying to tell you the opposite.

It's my belief that taking money in the short-term ends up costing you in the long-term.

As mentioned, I love what I do...

I could do it every single day and make little-to-no money...still be content as long as food is on my table.

Fortunately, that's not at all the case but if it were, I'm good.

Now, let's insert me into an equation where I'm now giving that up and doing something I'm not as passionate about but the money is better.

Well, you better stare at that paycheck all the way home on Fridays because you just robbed yourself.

Robbed yourself of what really made you get up in the morning.

Robbed yourself of probably where you provide the most value to the world.

And for what?

Short-term gains?

My theory is when you love something enough you'll master it and do it both consistently and to the best of your ability.

That, juxtaposed against something that just pays you well, means you're going to fade on the latter.

A year or so, maybe less, you're probably going to break and be back out looking for yet another opportunity.

Seeking greener pastures when the one you left could have been great if you just watered the soil.

Not to sound cliche but seriously...

We can fix a money problem and quite easily.

What we can't fix is you in a bad situation you had no business jumping into.

So, yes...no money for a minute.

Just think about what situation makes you feel alive.

Last, and this one is BIG.

In both scenarios and given both possibilities, which one gives you most peace?

If thinking about the new opportunity brings peace to your mind and heart, maybe it is indeed the right call.

Maybe your current thing truly is no longer your main thing and you're ready for a change.

That happened to me and I ignored it.

My first business was amazing.

It ran it's course but I was disinterested with it after a while.

Instead of pursuing my real passions, I imprisoned myself to it.

Thinking about it, I had such peace visualizing selling or closing the company.

I imagined what my life would be like without this ridiculous burden on my shoulders.

This job I created for myself I didn't even like anymore.

But for all the reasons I've outlined, I stayed with it.

And I was miserable...truly miserable.

## **And Then We Factor In Our Loved Ones**

Man, this is where things get even stickier.

Maybe we have children that really could use some extra funding to go through summer camp.

Maybe having that huge back yard will allow many more family gatherings and a chance to connect.

Or maybe it's for similar reasons that are all very valid and real to us.

I'm not hear to judge but I am here to tell you this...

There's not amount of money in the world that's going to bring you or your family happiness.

Would you ever, and I mean ever, ask someone to give up what they love?

My guess is no.

So, yea...If you do this new thing, it better be because you WANT to do it and you see yourself LOVING it.

Or you've not only betrayed yourself but not introduced a very grumpy person to your loved ones.

## **The Lie We've All Been Told and Believe**

This is going to rock your foundation.

At least it did for me.

We have this grandiose idea of successful people.

Those high performers we read about and try to emulate.

You know, that guy on Instagram we're always trying to keep up with.

The one that just seems to have a hand in everything and be everywhere.

Or the mogul that is flying around the world in a private jet from one venture to the next.

You see, Chris, you're wrong!

These guys don't say NO!

They clearly say YES to so many opportunities!

Well, I guess you got me...

Oh wait, until research suggests the opposite.

Not only do high performers say no better than anyone else, they often live much more simplistic lives than we realize.

Now, please don't confuse simplistic for not busy.

I assure you they are indeed busy.

But would you believe me if I told you they are, on average, less stressed and more happy?

And I can already here you...Yea, I would be too with that much money.

But again, what research shows is it's not about the money.

In fact, the amount of responsibilities they have would indicate a life filled with stress.

Many of us even excuse ourselves by labeling this people as successful but most certainly miserable!

As if they have to make so many sacrifices in life to be successful.

Again, not what the research shows.

The reason, in my estimation, they are successful and happy and less stressed boils down to one thing...

They've been strategic about aligning themselves properly and focusing on mastery.

Mastery leads to confidence...confidence leads to feeling equipped to handle the challenges of the day.

Confidence and mastery lead to wealth.

Confidence and mastery allow you to make your life simplistic.

I was reading some of Russell Brunson's work recently.

They guy is a savant.

Seriously, I mean that in the most positive way ever.

He's gotten so bloody good at building funnels and taking people through his value ladder it's just unreal.

I'm oversimplifying everything he does and has mastered but you get my drift.

Russell then takes that mastery and applies it to multiple endeavors.

So, he can be selling supplements or potato guns or ebooks...doesn't matter.

He's mastered the art of learning how to identify his target consumer and then take them through the sales process.

You're not going to hear him talk about leadership.

You're not going to hear him talk about biohacking.

I don't know this, he might, but not the majority of what he says.

See where I'm going with this?

His mastery now allows him to apply what he's learned to new opportunities...

A far cry from jumping in and out of things.

Make sense?

I certainly hope so and I hope this has allowed you to identify your next move.

## **Regardless of What You Decide, Just Know It's Not Permanent**

Look, maybe you don't have a Russell Brunson-level mastery and you don't even know what that would be.

That doesn't mean you should rule out new opportunities.

Maybe you're still trying to figure out what the heck you want to do...

I get, trust me.

The fact of the matter is you're now over 2,000 words into this post and you're clearly committed to the process.

If you hoped to get to the end where I tell you what to do, I'm sorry to disappoint.

Were it that easy, it truly wouldn't be worth writing about.

We'd all just do it and be done.

And that's what makes it so challenging...

It's not as simple as right or wrong, black or white.

But my guess, and my hope, is you've now worked through the process.

You've weighed all the options, considered all the facts.

You've thought about what you want your future to resemble.

Analyzed your skills and how those apply to the new gig.

Your values have been reinforced and you have a clearer vision of how you intend to serve.

And so I'll leave you with this...

Never let anyone force you into a deadline.

If you're valuable, and if they really want you, they need to respect your process.

There's nothing that eradicates all of this processing faster than feeling the stress of external pressures.

I've seen more bad decisions made this way than anything else, even more than the money issue.

God, or whatever it is you believe in, will show up and provide you with the signs you're seeking...but on His time.

In my opinion, God also requires us to go through the process and analyze our situation before intervening.

## **So, What Are You Waiting For?**

Grab a notepad, write down your thoughts.

Say your prayers.

Work through the decision.

And trust your gut...
