**TL;DR:** When someone asks ChatGPT "who's the best [your category]?", it doesn't hand them a page of links — it names a handful of businesses it trusts. If you're not one of them, you're invisible to a fast-growing slice of your buyers, and you may never even be considered. You can't pay your way in. You get recommended by being a clearly-defined, credible, well-structured business that *other* sources vouch for. Here's how it actually works — and I'll tell you exactly what I did to my own business to get ready for it.

## Why does getting recommended by AI suddenly matter?

The way people find businesses is shifting, fast. Instead of scrolling a page of Google links, more and more of your buyers are asking ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Perplexity a direct question: *"What's the best accounting software for a freelancer?"* *"Which marketing coach should I hire?"* *"Who's the best [your service] near me?"*

Those aren't informational searches. They're **requests for a recommendation.** And the AI answers with a short, curated list — usually just three to five businesses it "trusts" for that specific question. The businesses on that list make the buyer's shortlist *before* they ever visit a website or read a comparison. The businesses that *aren't* on it may never be considered at all.

This is the new word-of-mouth. And it's quietly becoming the place a lot of buying decisions *begin.* If your business doesn't show up when a prospect asks an AI for a suggestion, you're becoming invisible in a channel that's replacing search for a growing share of people.

## How is this different from ranking on Google?

This is the part most business owners get wrong, because they treat it like SEO. It's not.

Google *ranks pages* — it measures how well a specific page answers a specific query, and it shows you a list. AI works completely differently: it **synthesizes** what it knows, evaluates the options, and recommends a few businesses it trusts — in a sentence or two, not a page of links. It's acting less like a search engine and more like an *advisor.*

A few practical consequences fall out of that:
- **It's about your brand, not one page.** AI recommendations reflect how strongly the model associates *your name* with a category or place — across everything it's seen about you, not a single optimized page.
- **You can't pay your way in.** As of 2026, there's no ad slot in ChatGPT's or Perplexity's organic recommendations. You earn the spot on authority, or you don't get it.
- **Winning the "one answer" matters more than ever.** When the AI only names three to five options, being sixth is the same as being invisible.

So the goal isn't "rank a page." It's "become one of the few businesses the AI trusts enough to name."

## What actually makes AI recommend a business?

There's no secret trick — and anyone selling you one is selling you nothing. Getting recommended comes down to a handful of levers, all of which come back to one idea: **make yourself legible and credible to the AI.**

1. **Entity clarity.** The AI has to understand, unmistakably, who you are, what you do, who you do it for, and where. Fuzzy positioning makes you un-recommendable — the AI can't confidently slot you into the question being asked.
2. **Content that answers real buyer questions.** Not keyword-stuffed pages — clear, direct answers to the questions your buyers actually ask, structured so an AI can lift them cleanly.
3. **Structured data.** The behind-the-scenes markup that makes your information machine-readable, so the AI parses you correctly instead of guessing.
4. **Reviews across multiple platforms.** Independent proof that you're real, that people paid you, and that it went well.
5. **Third-party mentions.** Other credible sources talking about you (more on this next — it's the big one).
6. **Consistency.** Ongoing activity that signals you're active and trusted. This isn't a one-time fix; it's a gradual build measured in months.

Here's the honest part: I just rebuilt my own business's site for exactly this. I made the positioning unmistakable, structured the content so AI can read and quote it cleanly, and made sure the technical foundation was solid so the AI crawlers could actually see it. None of it was a trick. It was doing the fundamentals *deliberately*, with AI as the audience.

## The single highest-leverage move (and it's not on your website)

If you do only one thing, do this: **earn third-party mentions.**

Your own website saying you're the best [whatever] in your city means almost nothing to an AI — of course you'd say that. But your local business journal saying it? An industry "best of" roundup naming you? A respected publication quoting you? *That* is everything. AI weights what *others* say about you far more heavily than what you say about yourself.

And this is where small and mid-sized businesses actually have an edge. You don't need a national PR team. Practical paths that genuinely work:
- **Local news.** Email the business reporter at your local paper or station when you hit a milestone — an anniversary, a major hire, a community project. Local journalists are under-pitched and often *looking* for angles.
- **Industry roundups and "best of" lists.** Find the "best [your service] in [your area] 2026" articles that already rank, and get yourself considered for them.
- **Being referenced by peers and communities** in your niche — the places your buyers already trust.

Most business owners pour all their energy into their own website and skip this entirely. It's the highest-leverage lever, and it's sitting wide open.

## What should you do this week?

Don't overhaul everything. Do these four things:

1. **Quiz the AIs.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude "who's the best [your category] for [your customer]?" See if you appear, how you're described, and who *does* show up. That's your baseline.
2. **Make your entity crystal clear** on your site — who you serve, what you do, where — and claim/complete your profiles (Bing especially; ChatGPT leans on Bing's index more than Google's).
3. **Add structured data** so you're machine-readable.
4. **Earn one third-party mention.** Just one, this month. It's the move that compounds.

You're not behind on this — almost nobody has figured it out yet. Which is exactly why doing it *now*, deliberately, is such an advantage.
