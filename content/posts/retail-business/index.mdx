## **Seeking Investment Dollars Should Never Be Your Default**

You should see the level of frustration that takes over me when I hear entrepreneurs talk about needing investment dollars to "get started."

Trust me, if you say that phrase to me it's likely my skin just crawled.

Now, I'm not hear to tell you investment money is bad...

In fact, I waited wayyy too long in my first business to seek outside capital.

We had scaled to multiple seven figures in a very low margin business and I was running myself beyond dry to be the glue and hold it all together.

There are talented people in the world that could have easily made the journey much more enjoyable and more importantly, even more successful.

I opted against it until I could do no more on my own.

My body was literally giving out and my Doctor was scaring me with that "C" word none of us want to hear...definitely not at the ripe old age of 30.

But, when I hear stories like my featured guest today, Ms. Kelly Connolly, I'm reminded why I still believe in holding out on cap raises...just for a little while.

You see, when you scramble for money just to get your idea started, I think you miss out on an amazing opportunity to learn and grow.

There's something to be said about validating your idea before you just go off and pitch it to someone in hopes of a check.

I always try to relate things back to basic human psychology because I think all things get magnified in an entrepreneurial venture.

If you've got some daddy issues from childhood, the stress of a startup is going to poke at that button on a regular basis.

Maybe you'll think you're never good enough or no matter what you accomplish it's not enough.

Or maybe you've got some perfectionist tendencies and you just can't sell something until it's perfect (ie. see eMyth bakery story).

So, let's talk about how that applies to an early stage startup with a reference you will most assuredly resonate with.

How much did you learn when your parents just coughed over an allowance without requiring anything in return?

We all know the kid.

The one that drove all the newest and nicest cars, wore all the latest brands and got to go to Rated R movies while your evil parents made you stick to the latest Disney flick until you were old enough to literally go fight in a war.

Where's that kid now?

I don't want to speak in generalities but it's highly likely that was the pinnacle of their life.

They probably became entitled and have unrealistic expectations of what the world is about.

You see, it's not about the money.

Most parents can afford to chalk up the five bucks for you to grab lunch at Chick-Fil-A with your friends after school but it's more so teaching you the "value of a dollar" and the benefit of hard work.

There's something to be said about putting in the work and being rewarded for it.

When you skip those steps and jump straight to the reward, I think you're setting yourself up for failure, just like the kid that got everything they wanted.

## **And So You Learn Through Your Struggles...**

I've never been a parent so I have no idea how it must feel to watch your child struggle.

Particularly if you have the "remedy" for that particular struggle.

But, let me ask you something...

When's the last time you looked back at the most positive time of your life and said "Man, this amazing time in my life really pushed me to grow"?

Now, I'm not saying approach life as a masochist but my guess is that's probably not a statement you could align with.

We grow when we're under pressure.

We grow when our backs are against the wall.

When we have to make real sacrifices.

When our pain threshold is pushed to limits we didn't know possible.

And that's okay...

In fact, that's the good stuff if you temper it wisely.

Because for every late night and hella problem I've ever had to solve in my own businesses, I knew the solution fell square on my shoulders.

What happened was I now relate way more to people twice my age than most my own age.

And that's not to be critical, I assure you.

But it is a great measuring stick to see the tangible results of that pressure cooker.

And I don't think you get that when some rich guy just floats you a blank check and tells you to go have fun.

In fact, it's often I see a perpetual cycle of going back to the well for more...just as the kid that got everything he wanted.

And I think that's why you're starting to see this notion of "ideas not being worth shi*t" being more prevalent than ever.

Investors want to see traction...and I don't blame them.

Now, for the sake of the discussion and to (again) not speak in generalities, I will say that SOME businesses just require capital.

Uber doesn't become Uber by bootstrapping their way to revolutionizing transportation.

SpaceX doesn't send missiles to Mars on Elon Musk's personal credit card.

But what I'm finding more and more as I work with clients in the tech space is they are getting their asses handed to them in other ways.

It's why I'm involved with Accelerator Programs like Founder Institute.

We send them through literal boot camp.

We kick their tails every week and tell them how and where they need to grow & improve.

One of my favorite graduates from the program was a woman that decided to turn her knitting passion into a business.

I'll be transparent and say I didn't think she was going to complete the program.

I say that only because the program is intense and she seemed like she was in a good place in her life.

A solid job, a loving family, etc, etc.

In my experience, that type of person is usually too comfortable to go out and make the sacrifices necessary to launch a successful startup.

As the weeks passed, I noticed she just kept getting better and better.

Her commitment was stronger and slowly she became my favorite student.

By the end of the 3.5 month program, she gave her 60 second pitch to literal perfection.

I gave her a 5 out of 5, the first time I had ever done that...EVER.

She came over to me with what appeared to be a tear in her eye and said "you have no idea what that means coming from you."

Now, she's on her way to raise her seed round and that program has an 80% success rate.

Meaning, 80% of it's graduates (globally) are still in business.

I realized the reason she had a tear in her eye was because we had effectively and synthetically re-created the real world struggle she was going to face.

She put in the work and she EARNED my 5 rating.

When she now pitches investors, she'll 100% get my vote of confidence even though she has zero customers and has yet to generate a single dollar.

Why?

Because she's paid her dues and earned it.

I tell you that story because I want to be fair and say there's more than one way to skin a cat, so to speak.

You don't HAVE to go generate seven figures to warrant attention from an investor.

But, just having an idea, my lovely friend, is not enough...

At least in my book.

## **Your Business is A Child**

If you take a moment to listen to this pod episode with my incredible guest, Kelly Connolly, you'll quickly learn we both ironically use the same metaphor to describe your business.

It's an infant before it becomes a child, then a teen and finally a fully functioning adult...or so we hope.

While I though it was indeed bizarre she used the same metaphor it also reiterated to me just how effective it is at describing reality.

That said, let's go back to the example we were using before.

We've all seen the parent that is really disinterested in being a good parent and just expects the school to raise their child.

They may even cough up the extra money to send them to private school.

I don't know about you but like five faces immediately pop up in my own head.

The parent thinks they can just skip steps and cut corners.

But the reality of the situation is there is no viable substitute for an involved and committed parent.

And so the child usually acts out and lacks discipline.

Their grades suffer and as they get older it becomes more and more evident and severe.

It's literally the same dichotomy in your startup.

Too many startup founders are just trying to skip steps.

They want their company to "hire the help they need" when in reality it's barely even crawling yet...figuratively speaking.

They're trying to cut corners but the business demands your full time attention.

It's not ready to sustain itself and provide all of it's unique value to the world, let alone hire anyone else.

It's probably barely even paying you!

My first company didn't write it's first official paycheck to me until we had hit $500,000 in revenue!

Crazy, right?

Now, a lot of this was built around fear but in my mind I was doubling down on investing in the company.

I would pay myself small distribution checks to cover my basic needs but everything else went back into growth.

It's debatable but it's probably, and most likely, the reason we did grow from launch to seven figures in just 36 months.

I treated the business like my baby.

I put it's needs ahead of that of my own.

I made the necessary sacrifices.

What's more is that's also EXACTLY what my guest did as well.

Kelly had earned her way into a very nice home in Nashville, TN.

She also knew she needed the money to start her own business.

And so she swallowed her pride, sold her home, and did what was necessary for her idea to become a reality.

The results, as you'll hear in this episode, were more or less the same.

Rampant and abundant success.

And that's no coincidence.

## **So, Where Do We Go From Here?**

Brick and mortar businesses are tough.

What's going to make someone come into your retail store over the next fancy shop?

Moreover, what's stopping them from just buying that item they saw online?

You see, it's one thing to have a product but that doesn't mean they will come.

And this is where creating an experience comes into play.

Standing for something more than just selling clothes.

If that's your goal, I can almost assure you you're not going to steal any loyal shoppers from Ross or TJ Maxx.

But doing something for a reason, a reason that is unique to you, will establish a level of polarity that makes people resonate and support your cause.

This, with any other business for that matter, is what turns interested potential customers into super fans.

Let's examine Apple.

Apple had lengthy internal debates about whether to make the Apple logo upside down to the user or the onlooker.

If you're using a MacBook right now, you've probably already folded your device down to see which route they opted for.

What they found was that Apple wasn't just about buying the latest and greatest technology.

In fact, pound for pound, it's arguable that they even do have the superior product when you do a quick comparison at your local Best Buy.

But what they realized is that having an Apple device was a status symbol.

It said something about who you are as a person.

And so much of their branding and marketing is built around this idea of you don't have THE product until you have an Apple product.

I certainly know I feel that pressure when I roll in with my HP Envy to a room full of entrepreneurs, all of whom have Apple products.

I'm the weird guy with an HP.

And my HP is VASTLY superior to their MacBooks on nearly every level.

What's more is I paid about half to one third of what they paid, retail.

But again, Apple has instilled that notion in our brains and it was by design.

One of the reasons I believe my guest today, Kelly, was able to not just grow with zero outside capital but to do so at an extraordinary rate is because there was substance behind her cause.

There was a reason she felt so compelled to do what she does.

And that mission is evident in her branding.

It's even subtly hidden in the name of the company.

I've not personally been to one of her stores but I did have the privilege of talking business with her outside of our recording.

She knows her people and her people know her.

I would be hard pressed to say they aren't going completely out of their way to shop at her stores.

And that, my friend, isn't bought by investors.

## **This Isn't the Space I Play In**

I love having guests on my show of whom I've never experienced their particular vertical.

Needless to say, my business prowess has yet to reach the specialized female retail niche.

But what amazes me is when I talk to these founders throughout a variety of industries is that much of what I've done and taught applies across the board.

The trade itself is inherently different but the same basic principles remain the same.

It's a hands on type of business, not unlike most brick and mortars.

Truth be told, you'd be shocked to learn just how much of a hands on business this online thing is for me.

News Alert: This is ACTUALLY me writing this blog.

And that goes for every single multi-thousand word blog I put out.

And truth be told, that IS indeed my voice on the podcast.

Much goes into the work behind the scenes.

And for that, I am ill-equipped to tell you the specificities as it relates to running a brick and mortar retail store.

But here's what I do know and what I think your major takeaway should be here with me today.

Entrepreneurship is a lifestyle, not a career.

I say it all the time but it never rings more true than it does right now.

All of this talk about doing things yourself and making sacrifices.

Struggling to reach your goals and learning along the way.

I'm literally just envisioning Kelly sitting in her apartment a few short years ago and personally doing the work to try and figure this whole thing out.

I know her pain, anyone that's gone through it knows immediately what I'm talking about.

I know the fear she must have felt when thinking about the possibilities things might not work out as planned.

And I also know that's most likely why you're seeking the investment money.

C'mon, we're now friends here.

It's not just that we doubt our ability but more so the what-if's.

We're wired to avoid pain.

You don't walk up to a hot stove and look forward to putting your hand on it.

Leaving your comfy 9 to 5 to venture off into the unknown is against your DNA.

It also goes against at least 20 years of mental conditioning.

## **You're Afraid, We're All Afraid...And That's Normal**

The investor route bails you out of your fears.

You have nothing to lose!

And they're just a bunch of rich guys, this is what they do!

Now, I recognize we don't actively think that but if we're being truly honest that's definitely where it comes from.

I recently went to Seattle for an Intensive with Keith Krance and his amazing team.

And boy was it ever INTENSE.

One of my major takeaways was the depths of thinking they go to in order to get in the minds of their consumer.

I mean, this was beyond a science, to say the least.

And so I personally realized that I was largely only meeting my audience at a skin deep level.

And so I would usually end this message with just giving you all the reasons why you should or shouldn't seek investors.

But I'm no longer letting you off the hook.

So, let's go back to my original statement.

The WHY we want investment dollars right out of the gates:

**Reason #1:** If someone successful believes in our idea and is willing to invest in it, it's now worth pursuing.

**Reason #2:** As previously mentioned, it lets our fear off the hook because we have nothing to lose but someone else's money.

I'll give you a few seconds to wrestle with those...

I'd love to see someone attempt to dispute either of those.

I'm constantly emailed about investing in someone's startup and I've now seen these two happen on countless occasions.

One day I'll go into way more detail on this.

Either way, the point is we have to get down to the WHY before we even think about outside capital.

Until you're able to do that and do so rationally with an actual execution strategy on how you intend to get a return on the investment, you have no business even thinking about asking for money in my opinion.

## **Start Slow, This Isn't a Race**

I'm going to leave you with this today.

And it's not at all what you want to hear, I'm all too aware.

But Rome wasn't built in a day and nor will your startup.

These amazing success stories we see blasted all over Forbes are just anomalies.

But what you don't see are the thousands of companies that failed miserably alongside them.

When you take investment money, the clock is ticking.

Investors expect their money back and usually within 3-5 years.

Guess what you do in 3-5 years?

Find a BIGGER investor or VC/PE firm to take your next round of funding.

Guess what you do in the next few years?

Find yet ANOTHER firm to take your next round of funding.

And so the circus continues.

That's how it works, folks.

These investors aren't your friends...not entirely, at least.

They are business people that expect you to make money.

And they expect you to listen to what they have to say about it in the process.

Without delving into too many details for our topic today, I can tell you that's not a race you want to run until you're ready for it.

And if you can avoid it altogether and build a nice little lifestyle business for yourself, more power to you!

In fact, the internet has never provided a better opportunity to do just that.

Keeping your overhead low and starting up an ECommerce business is totally a viable path.

And if your successes reach a certain crescendo, maybe you decide it would be fun to have actual locations.

But in my view, the world of investment bucks is changing.

And changing fast.

As of May, 2016, you didn't even have to be an accredited investor to put your money behind a startup.

Crowdfunding has become an amazing way for people to get startup money.

And Institutionalized funding is starting to recognize this evolution.

In many cases, they are now ONLY focused on later stage companies.

Meaning, you're going to have to be doing $3-5MM ARR to even warrant their consideration.

Pretty nuts, right?

So, here's the deal...I could go on for days.

But just remember this.

There's no shame in the journey.

In fact, the journey is where the good stuff happens.

I wouldn't be who I am as an entrepreneur without the journey.

Enjoy it, take your time, bask in it...

Because, just like raising children (I surmise), one day they are going to be all grown up and not need you anymore.

That is, if you do it right.
