I want to give you my honest read on what's coming with AI — and what I'd be doing about it if I were you.

I'm calling it a survival guide, which is a little dramatic. But I do want to prepare you for what I see ahead. I've simplified my thinking into six eras, and I'll walk through each one with the moves I'd make as a business owner — because that's the lens that matters here.

One thing up front: these are *predictions*. I could be wrong, and in some cases I hope I am. But this is how I see it playing out, and even if the timeline shifts, the direction feels clear. Let's get into it.

## The 6 Eras of AI (My Prediction)

Think of these as a rough sequence, not exact dates. Some will overlap, and the later ones are the most speculative.

1. **Adoption** — AI becomes woven into daily life.
2. **Adapt** — business owners infuse AI into how they actually operate. *(We're here.)*
3. **Expansion** — friction collapses, and scale and abundance explode.
4. **The AGI Crisis** — general intelligence arrives and reshapes the labor economy.
5. **The Energy Crisis** — the power demands of all this force a hard reckoning.
6. **The New Paradigm** — work becomes more optional and meaning-centered.

The two I'm genuinely excited about are Adapt and Expansion. The two I'm most concerned about are the AGI and Energy crises — the messy transition in the middle. Let me take them in order.

## Where Are We Now? The Adoption and Adapt Eras

We're sitting right between Adoption and Adapt.

**Adoption** is mostly behind us. For the first time, Google's search numbers are softening — you hear "just ask [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com)" or "ask [Grok](https://grok.com)" where you used to hear "Google it." Clients tell me they can't remember how they worked before AI. It's becoming as fundamental as the phone in your pocket.

**Adapt** is where the action is now — and where I'd put my focus if I were you. This is the era where producers (business owners) stop using AI as a novelty fact-checker and start running their businesses with it. Market research, content, client delivery, operations — the things you used to grind through by hand.

Here's the part I'd internalize: how many times have you said, *"there's so much I don't want to do, but it just has to get done"*? That era is ending. You can hand a language model your promotion and ask for ten emails, and seconds later they exist. They might need editing — but "getting it done" was the heavy lift for most people, and that lift is largely gone. **You + AI** is the new operating unit.

One more reframe that shapes how my team operates: we're not early adopters, we're **timely adapters**. I'm not resourced to compete with the labs building frontier models, and I'm not trying to. What I *can* do is move decisively the moment a new paradigm is established. You can too. You don't have to be first. You have to be timely.

## Why the Next 2–5 Years Are a Once-in-a-Generation Window

The Expansion era is what I'm most fired up about — and it's why I'd move with urgency right now.

When you remove the friction — automate the busywork, build your systems, deploy your AI as a research partner and assistant — your capacity to grow goes up dramatically. In my view, the next 2–5 years are the biggest window business owners and producers may ever get to win. We're treating it exactly that way internally: two-to-five-year windows, maximum urgency, do the most we can while the window is open.

This isn't about hype. It's arithmetic. The operators who build now will be operating at a level their competitors can't match by the time everyone else catches on. If there's a single takeaway from this whole post, it's this: **this is the era to build, and the cost of waiting has never been higher.**

## What Is the AGI Crisis, and Should You Be Worried?

This is the first of the two eras I have real concerns about — and it's worth understanding rather than fearing.

AGI is artificial *general* intelligence. What we mostly have today is narrow AI, trained for specific tasks. AGI is the next tier — a system that can reason, deduce, and think across domains much more like a human. It's not a light switch; it's a slow evolution that's already underway, and it demands staggering amounts of computing power.

My honest prediction: as AGI matures, it displaces a large share of desk-based jobs. Hands-on and skilled-trade work — and some healthcare — likely have longer runways. Society will have to grapple with the transition, and ideas like universal basic income or a "tech tax" on the companies driving this will enter the conversation as real policy options.

I'd compare it to when machines took over plowing fields and factory line work. Terrifying in the moment; obvious in hindsight. The unknown is what's scary. The honest answer is that none of us knows exactly how this lands — but understanding the shape of it now is how you position instead of panic.

## The Energy Crisis Era

Here's the constraint nobody talks about enough: all of this — AGI, robotics, the compute behind it — runs on enormous amounts of energy we don't currently produce.

If autonomous systems are running your local grocery store, they don't take smoke breaks; they take *charge* breaks. The processing power required for the next wave is on a scale our current grid simply can't supply. So I think a massive energy build-out is inevitable — and that this era may actually arrive *before* or alongside the AGI crisis. Either way, it's part of the same turbulent transition.

## The New Paradigm: A "Return to Real"

On the far side of the transition, I think we land somewhere genuinely better — though not a utopia.

If costs collapse and abundance rises the way I expect, work becomes far more optional. You could do the thing you actually love — write, build, create, teach — without the constant pressure of "if I don't land a client this month, I can't make rent." I call it a *return to real*: we weren't put here just to pay bills for fifty years. I don't think it's paradise — I think we'll wrestle with real questions about meaning and purpose when so much is handled for us. But compared to the path we're currently on, I'll take it.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **How will AI change business over the next 5 years?**

In my view, the next few years are the "Adapt" and "Expansion" eras — where business owners infuse AI into operations, content, research, and delivery, removing the friction that used to cap their growth. I believe this creates an unusually large window for producers to scale, before a more turbulent transition arrives later. These are predictions, not certainties.

### **What is AGI and how is it different from current AI?**

AGI (artificial general intelligence) is AI that can reason and think across many domains more like a human, versus today's narrow AI that's trained for specific tasks. It's expected to arrive gradually rather than overnight, and it requires enormous computing power and energy — which is why energy capacity is so tied to the AGI conversation.

### **Should business owners be worried about AI replacing jobs?**

There's real reason to take the transition seriously — many desk-based roles are likely to change significantly, while hands-on and skilled-trade work may have longer runways. But for business owners specifically, this period is also an opportunity: those who adopt AI to expand output and capacity can position themselves well before the bigger shifts arrive.

### **What should I do to prepare my business for AI?**

Be a timely adapter: build AI into how you actually operate now — your systems, content, research, and client delivery — rather than waiting. The practical starting points (architecture, thought partners, connected tools) are covered in detail in my other posts. The goal is to use this window to build capacity before the transition.

### **Is this guaranteed to happen the way you describe?**

No. This is my honest prediction of where things are heading, and I openly say I could be wrong — especially on timing and the later eras. The value is in thinking ahead so you can position now, not in treating any forecast as a certainty.
